# CHARTS

Deep dive into Beat Saber (BSMG wiki), osu!standard/mania/taiko ranking criteria, StepMania/ITG pattern theory, Clone Hero/Harmonix authoring, and the cosmovibe + exceed7 notecharting guides, then translated all of it into a concrete, numeric generator rule set for CHOP.

The single most important translation: **Beat Saber's parity rule inverts for a thumb.** In VR, a 135°+ angle change between swings is what AVOIDS a reset (0° and 45° force one); the sabers flow when the hand reverses forehand↔backhand. On a touchscreen the thumb has no forehand/backhand — it has a stroke and a lift. A 180° reversal (up→down, left→right) is the FREE motion (one continuous stroke, no lift); a same-direction repeat (up→up) is the expensive one (it's a jack — you must lift and re-place). So for CHOP the constraint is a MINIMUM angle change, not a maximum, and the cheapest chart is a straight oscillation on one axis. That one inversion, plus "the cut IS the movement" (a DOWN cut leaves the thumb low, so the next note belongs in the bottom row), turns flow from a hand-authored art into something a generator can obey mechanically.

I also read <repo>/public/chop/js/music.js, so every number below is snapped to the music you already built: **136 BPM, 16th-step sequencer, 40-bar cycle (INTRO 8 · BUILD 8 · DROP 16 · BREAK 8) = 70.6s**. Derived grid: 16th = 110.3ms, 8th = 220.6ms, dotted-8th = 330.9ms, quarter = 441.2ms, bar = 1764.7ms. Every timing rule below is expressed in those units so the generator can work in integer 16th-steps and never touch floating-point ms.

One structural bug found in passing: the existing arrangement ENDS on the BREAK (bars 32-39, the lowest-energy section). A 70-second level that is also a "sealed block" trophy must end on its peak. Recommendation #4 reorders it.

## Findings

- PARITY, AND WHY IT INVERTS FOR A THUMB. BSMG's basic-mapping page gives the canonical angle table for a saber: 0 degrees change = reset (bad), 45 = reset (bad), 90 = maybe, 135+ = no reset (good). Parity is 'the forehand and backhand nature of a block' (BSMG glossary), and 'adapting your creative vision to keep parity consistently is almost always better than breaking parity' (BSMG intermediate). The physical basis is that a forehand swing ENDS where a backhand swing STARTS, so alternating costs zero setup. A thumb on glass has the same property with different geometry: an up-swipe ends with the thumb high and the pad still down, so a down-swipe is a free continuation of the same stroke; an up-swipe followed by another up-swipe requires a lift and re-place, which is exactly osu!mania's 'jack' (3+ consecutive notes in the same column, named after the jackhammer, 'typically snapped at 1/4 or faster' and universally treated as the expensive pattern). Conclusion: CHOP's parity-preserving transition is the 180-degree reversal; the 0-degree repeat is the reset. Sources: https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-mapping.html , https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/intermediate-mapping.html , https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/glossary.html , https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/Beatmap/Pattern/osu!mania/Jack
- NOTE DENSITY, WITH REAL NUMBERS, AND THE ONE-HAND CONVERSION. BSMG publishes NPS bands per difficulty: Easy 0.82-2.29, Normal 1.05-3.41, Hard 1.73-5.22, Expert 2.42-7.81, Expert+ 3.21-10.66. Those are TOTALS across two sabers. One thumb equals one saber, so the per-limb bands are Easy 0.41-1.15, Normal 0.53-1.71, Hard 0.87-2.61, Expert 1.21-3.91, Expert+ 1.61-5.33 notes/second. This is the cleanest available calibration for a one-thumb game and it says a 2.6 NPS CHOP chart is 'one-handed Beat Saber Expert' — the correct ceiling for a 30-50yo phone audience, not a floor. BSMG also gives the downmapping ratio: 'reduce the overall note density of each difficulty by approximately 20% as you work your way down the spread.' Source: https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-mapping.html , https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/downmapping.html
- THE 115-POINT SCORE IS A CHARTING CONSTRAINT, NOT JUST A SCORING ONE. BSMG's ranking guide breaks the 115 down as 70 points for a 100-degree approach swing, 30 points for a 60-degree follow-through, and 15 for cutting the block in half. 87% of a note's score is therefore EARNED OUTSIDE THE HIT INSTANT — before and after. Any chart that packs notes so tightly that the player cannot complete a full stroke and follow-through is mathematically capped below 100%, which reads to the player as 'the game is cheating me.' The chart must budget stroke room, not just reaction time. Combo ladder is 1x -> 2x -> 4x -> 8x and is HALVED (not zeroed) on a miss. Source: https://bsmg.wiki/ranking-guide.html
- THE FORBIDDEN-PATTERN LIST IS EXPLICIT AND MOSTLY PORTABLE. BSMG names these as hard bans: handclaps (blocks pointing at each other in close proximity), hammer hits (a directional block pointing at a bomb on the same plane), disembodied notes (blocks hidden behind walls), rapid wall dodge and 'danger dash' (forcing the player to cross their whole playspace at speed), hidden blocks (stacked or inside walls), hitbox abuse (insufficient swing room between adjacent blocks), and vision blocks. Situational-only: triangle patterns (uncomfortable resets, acceptable at Hard, avoid Expert+), flicks (2+ same-color blocks at 1/4+), excessive towers (3-4 successive same-color blocks 'to prevent overemphasis and fatigue'), sky streams (repetitive top-row usage causes shoulder strain), awkward curves, and 'visual clutter' (directional blocks pointing toward or away from dot blocks 'lack functional purpose'). Source: https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-mapping.html
- THE VISION-BLOCK RULE IS THE MOST TRANSFERABLE READABILITY LAW. BSMG best-practices: '95% of the time, you should not place blocks in the middle two sections of the grid,' because the middle columns are 'face notes' that occlude what is behind them. The safe rule is stated as a time window: 'If you place down a face block, avoid placing other blocks in that half of the grid afterwards until the half jump duration has passed.' The downmapping page gives the on-screen complexity budget in the same units: unique patterns within one half-jump-distance interval should be at most 2-3 on Easy and 4-5 on Normal. Sources: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bsmg/beat-saber-community-wiki/master/mapping/best-practices.md , https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/downmapping.html
- REACTION TIME HAS PUBLISHED BOUNDS. BSMG intermediate: standard Expert+ reaction time is 500-600ms; 'don't use below 350 ms or above 1200 ms as default.' NJS should be 'as low as possible without clutter' (baseline 16 for Expert+, range 14-18, never below 8 or above 25), with jump distance 24-30 across the spread. Faster maps warrant shorter reaction time; LARGE MOVEMENTS WARRANT LONGER. That last clause is the one that matters for a 3x2 grid on a 6-inch screen: a full-width lane jump needs more approach time than an in-place reversal. Source: https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/intermediate-mapping.html
- RESETS MUST BE PAID FOR IN TIME AND TELEGRAPHED. BSMG: 'Resets do have their uses if you give the player enough time to reset their hands back to neutral position and, more importantly, clearly communicate to them that they should reset.' The measured placement rule for a bomb reset is 'closer to the notes that come after than to the notes that come before. Normally at 3/4 or 2/3 of the distance' — a 1/2-distance reset is called out as 'Too close! Jerky uncomfortable motion.' Also: many new mappers use parity breaks 'to simulate the feel of drumming... but drums have rebound when you hit them and imaginary computer blocks don't.' That is precisely the trap CHOP falls into if it maps every kick to a DOWN cut. Source: https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-mapping.html
- EMPHASIS MUST BE RATIONED, AND THE COMMUNITY STATES IT AS A LAW. BSMG intermediate: 'If everything is emphasized, nothing is emphasized.' The emphasis ladder is explicit — quiet sections get one block; two different instruments or a 'bigger' sound gets a double or a stack; a double tower (six blocks) is 'reserved for largest sounds only.' Spacing is the second emphasis axis, with a published scale: 0 spacing (inline) = low effort and hard to read, 1 = standard/low emphasis, 2 = 'jumps'/medium emphasis, 3+ = large emphasis requiring arm movement. And the consistency contract: 'mapping the same sound with the same weight when it appears.' Source: https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/intermediate-mapping.html
- SWING SPEED HAS A PUBLISHED COMFORT BAND (eBPM). BSMG intermediate quantifies effective swing rate: a 1/4 stream is 1x BPM, one-hand jumps are 2x BPM, 1/3 jumps 1.5x. Comfort bands: 100-150 eBPM is natural body movement, 150-200 moderately intense, 200-300 challenging for most players, 300+ extreme where 'speed becomes the primary difficulty.' At CHOP's 136 BPM, straight 8ths on one thumb = 272 eBPM equivalent motions per minute, which lands in the 'challenging for most players' band — correct for a top tier, wrong as a default. Source: https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/intermediate-mapping.html
- OSU!TAIKO IS THE BEST MODEL FOR VOICE-TO-NOTE-TYPE MAPPING, AND IT IS PRESCRIPTIVE. The ranking criteria states the assignment rule outright: 'Heavier/lower tones should be set as don and a lighter/higher tones should be set as kat,' and every note 'must have its colour clearly distinguishable from the previous and upcoming notes.' It also caps pattern lengths per difficulty, all normalized to ~180 BPM 4/4: Kantan 1/1 patterns not longer than 7 notes; Futsuu 1/2 not longer than 7 (5 if lowest diff), 1/3 not longer than 2; Muzukashii 1/4 not longer than 5, 1/6 not longer than 4; Oni 1/4 not longer than 9, 1/8 not longer than 2. Minimum note spacing by difficulty: Kantan/Futsuu 1/2 beat, Futsuu 1/3, Muzukashii 1/6, Oni 1/8. Source: https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/Ranking_criteria/osu!taiko
- TAIKO ALSO CODIFIES MANDATORY REST, WHICH IS THE THING AUTO-GENERATORS ALWAYS OMIT. Rest moments are REQUIRED every 16-20 beats on Oni and every 32-36 beats on lower difficulties, with minimum rest lengths of 3/1 (Kantan), 2/1 (Futsuu), 3/2 or three consecutive 1/1 (Muzukashii), and 1/1 (Oni). osu!standard says the same thing differently for its lowest difficulty: 'Include a 1/1 or longer gap in rhythm for every two measures of gameplay.' And it caps accents: finishers 'must not be used in any 1/4 patterns or faster' (Muzukashii) and, when attached to a 1/4 pattern on Oni, 'must only be used at the end of that pattern and be of the opposite colour.' Sources: https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/Ranking_criteria/osu!taiko , https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/Ranking_criteria/osu!
- OSU!MANIA GIVES HARD PER-DIFFICULTY CAPS THAT PORT DIRECTLY TO TIERS. Snap ceilings: Easy avoids 1/4 and faster, Normal avoids 1/6 and faster, Hard avoids 1/8 and faster, Insane unrestricted. Chord caps: Easy max 2 notes, Normal 2 (4K), Hard 3 (4K). Run caps: Easy 'avoid more than 5 consecutive 1/2 notes,' Normal same for 1/4, Hard 'long 1/4 streams need a 1/2 beat pause per musical phrase,' Insane 'avoid split rolls or 1/8 streams lasting longer than 4 beats.' Pattern-count caps elsewhere: 'avoid using more than 9 consecutive notes in a trill.' The mania mapping guide adds the pitch law — 'lower pitched sounds are mapped on the left side, the higher sounds on the right side' — and the consistency law: 'Do not use different kinds of patterns for exactly the same sound in your map.' Sources: https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/Ranking_criteria/osu!mania , https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/Guides/osu!mania_mapping_guide
- OSU!STANDARD TIES DENSITY TO MUSICAL INTENSITY AS A RANKING REQUIREMENT, NOT A SUGGESTION. Rhythm density by difficulty: Easy 'mostly 1/1, 2/1, or slower,' Normal 'mostly 1/1, occasional 1/2, or slower,' Hard 'mostly 1/2, occasional 1/4, or slower.' The intensity coupling: 'intense parts should have more rhythm density than calm parts, and intense parts should have higher jump distance than calm parts'; 'when the song gradually increases or decreases intensity, show a gradual progression of any elements like rhythm density, spacing, slider velocity.' And the anti-ambiguity rule: 'avoid visually similar spacing for different rhythms' — spacing variation for emphasis is encouraged 'only if these jumps are recognisably different from other rhythm gaps.' Source: https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/Ranking_criteria/osu!
- THE NOTECHARTING LITERATURE NAMES THE FEELING WE ARE CHASING AND THE ONE WE ARE AVOIDING. exceed7's crash course calls it 'norinori-ness': sparse isolated 16th notes 'feel awkward to play despite sounding engaging when listening,' because the brain generates 'imaginary beats' during passive listening that gameplay disrupts. It explicitly endorses STRATEGIC INACCURACY — 'omitting or adding technically inaccurate notes can enhance playability,' e.g. adding notes before significant moments 'creates better momentum than rigid accuracy.' It warns against 'long note syndrome' and the 'chordsmashing mess' that results from decorating every overlapping sound by rule, and recommends 'dry charting' — 'let the player feel it instead of cluttering the chart.' Most importantly for a chorus/drop: 'consistency is key' so players can maintain engagement, 'with technical sections reserved for transitions or conclusions.' The drop should be the most MEMORABLE section, not the hardest. Source: https://exceed7.com/native-audio/rhythm-game-crash-course/game-design-and-notecharting.html
- PITCH-TO-POSITION AND PATTERN-VARIATION RULES ARE CONSISTENT ACROSS COMMUNITIES. exceed7: 'Higher frequency sounds tend to go to the right or up' — align placement with melodic contour so ascending arpeggios flow left-to-right; osu!mania says the same. On variation: repeated patterns cohere when they represent 'the same meaning'; 'mirror patterns work when physical execution remains comparable, but arbitrary variation disrupts player association.' On accidental patterns: 'minimize unintentional mini-jacks or bracketing formations unless deliberately used for impact.' On ergonomics: 'jumps from upper-left to lower-right feel more taxing than left-right to up-down movements due to momentum differences' — i.e. axis-aligned motion is cheaper than diagonal, which is the same conclusion the parity inversion reaches from the other direction. Sources: https://exceed7.com/native-audio/rhythm-game-crash-course/game-design-and-notecharting.html , https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/Guides/osu!mania_mapping_guide
- MOTIF AND STRUCTURE: THE MAP MUST REUSE FIGURES BECAUSE THE MUSIC DOES. The osu! mapping-consistency material frames it as: songs are built on 'intervals and repetitive motives'; 'a map should probably reuse a lot of similar rhythm choices throughout its length,' the same rhythm repeated for 2 or 4 measures; and crucially 'the percussion is never random — outside from drum solos it's always consistent for at least 1x (and more often 2x) the timeframe found earlier. It is important to make your beatmap reflect this properly by not including random extra notes.' Sanctioned variation is one-axis: change the velocity, or the pattern, or mirror it — not all at once. Source: https://osu.ppy.sh/community/forums/topics/743288 (via search) and https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/Ranking_criteria/osu!
- STEPMANIA/ITG PATTERN THEORY GIVES THE VOCABULARY FOR 'WHY A TURN IS SPICY.' Streams alternate feet and preserve parity; CROSSOVERS deliberately subvert alternation and 'vastly expand a chart writer's range of pattern choices, providing ways to increase or decrease the intensity of phrases, highlight climaxes, and control player movement'; FOOTSWITCHES hit the same arrow twice with alternating feet. Brackets + footswitches + crossovers are the community's 'Big Three' technical techniques. The cosmovibe glossary adds the full pattern taxonomy CHOP needs names for: stream, streamlet (<16 notes), burst ('short but particularly fast, significantly harder than the parts of the chart near it'), trill, minitrill (<=5 notes), jack, minijack (<=4), roll (a repeating sequence like 1234123412341234), anchor, and PANNING ('any pattern that produces a feel of going from left to right or right to left'). Sources: https://stepmania.fandom.com/wiki/Pattern_for_Dance , https://www.cosmovibe.com/ncguide/glossary.html
- THE CHART IS AN AUTHORED POINT OF VIEW, WHICH IS EXACTLY WHAT AN AUTOMAPPER CANNOT FAKE. cosmovibe: 'A chart represents a listener's perspective of a song' — the chartmaker decides which layer the player hears by deciding which layer they PLAY. Quality charts 'flow well and highlight the song's particular features'; 'technicality' is defined as how well a chart follows mechanical rules of sync, transcription accuracy and consistency, while 'flow' is separately defined as 'how smooth a chart feels to play' — the two are orthogonal and a chart can pass technicality and still feel dead. Source: https://www.cosmovibe.com/ncguide/chartintro.html and /glossary.html
- AUTO-GENERATED CHARTS FAIL IN TWO SPECIFIC, DIAGNOSABLE WAYS. The Beat Sage critique is consistently (a) density holes — 'maps feel empty, with not nearly enough blocks and long gaps without blocks' — and (b) genre brittleness, 'only certain types of music work well with the generation algorithm.' The Dance Dance Convolution paper (Donahue, Lipton, McAuley, ICML 2017) decomposes charting into exactly the two subtasks a generator must solve separately: STEP PLACEMENT (when to place a note, conditioned on chart difficulty) and STEP SELECTION (which note), and finds that step selection needs a sequence model with memory because the correct next arrow depends on the pattern history, not just the audio — n-gram and fixed-window approaches are substantially worse. Translation: a per-onset independent decision function will always feel random; the direction of note n must be a function of notes n-1..n-k. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06891 , https://www.uploadvr.com/beat-sage-ai-beat-saber-custom/
- MOBILE FLICK PRECEDENT SAYS BE LENIENT ON DIRECTION, STRICT ON AXIS. Project Sekai's flicks are strictly up-only, and even a wrong-direction swipe 'as long as you swipe up' still awards a GREAT; BanG Dream allows flicks in any direction. Both are the top-grossing charts-and-flicks games on phones and both deliberately refuse to demand precise 4-way direction under pressure. Beat Blade: Dash Dance is the one-thumb-slash precedent — 'By using one thumb, you can chop the blocks and dodge the traps' — and it succeeds by keeping the lane count tiny and the direction vocabulary near-zero. CHOP is asking for more than any shipped mobile game asks, so the direction requirement has to be earned through tiering, not assumed. Sources: https://thejpfdude.wordpress.com/2020/10/05/rr-10-05-2020/ , https://rhythm-game-database.fandom.com/wiki/Beat_Blade:_Dash_Dance
- HUMAN INPUT CEILINGS ON GLASS. Sustained finger tapping on a touchscreen runs about 5-7 taps/second for average mobile users and 7-10 for competitive mobile gamers, with bursts to 12-18 only via multi-finger or alternating-finger technique; single-finger sustained is ~6-10 TPS. A directional SWIPE is strictly slower than a tap because it adds stroke distance plus a lift-and-reposition, and HCI work classifies a touch under 200ms as a 'short' interaction — 200ms is roughly the floor for a discrete deliberate gesture. Combined with the requirement that judgement windows must not overlap (min gap > 2x the outer timing window), this puts CHOP's absolute inter-gesture floor at ~200ms, which at 136 BPM is exactly the 8th note (220.6ms) and firmly excludes the 16th (110.3ms). Sources: https://cpsmeter.com/touchscreen-tap-speed/ , https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3613904.3642272 (tap-vs-swipe timing), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8651103/
- CHAINS/ARCS ARE THE SANCTIONED WAY TO CHART FASTER THAN THE LIMB. Beat Saber's arcs and chains exist precisely to reconcile musical density with physical limits: 'Arcs ease the swing requirements needed to fully score on connected blocks' and 'arcs magnetise to the player's sabers'; chains do the same. This is the mechanism that lets CHOP honour a 16th-note snare roll without asking for 9 gestures in a bar. Source: https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-mapping.html
- YOUR MUSIC ENGINE ALREADY FIXES THE GRID, AND IT HAS A STRUCTURAL PROBLEM. <repo>/public/chop/js/music.js is 136 BPM on a 16th-note scheduler with a 40-bar phrase cycle: sectionOf() gives INTRO bars 0-7, BUILD 8-15, DROP 16-31, BREAK 32-39. That is 70.59s — your ~70s level. The onset grid per bar (16th index 0-15) is: kick on 0/4/8/12 (intro only 0 and 8); snare on 4 and 12 plus a drop ghost snare on 10; closed hats on every even 16th with metal accents on 2/6/10/14 and an open hat on 14 (and 6 in the drop); ghost 16ths on 3/7/11/15 at L>=2; drop double-time hats on all odd 16ths at L>=3; bass on the quarters; pad on 0; stabs on 6/14 in the build and 2/6/10/14 in the drop; and a snare-roll FILL across 16ths 8-15 of every section's last bar. The problem: the level ENDS on the BREAK, the lowest-energy section, capped at intensity 2 by the arrangement floors. A one-block-one-level trophy run must peak at the end. ## Recommendations

- RULE 0 — THE PARITY INVERSION (adopt this first; everything else depends on it). For a thumb, the 180-degree reversal is FREE and the 0-degree repeat is the RESET. Encode a transition cost table keyed on the angle between consecutive required cut directions: 180deg (UP->DOWN, DOWN->UP, LEFT->RIGHT, RIGHT->LEFT) = cost 1.0, the flowing motion; 90deg (any vertical->horizontal or horizontal->vertical) = cost 1.5, a real turn; 0deg (same direction twice = a JACK) = cost 2.0, a reset that must be paid for in time. There is no 45deg case because we only have 4 directions — a deliberate simplification that makes the generator's job tractable. Every timing rule below is `requiredGap = tierBaseSnap * transitionCost + travelPenalty`, snapped UP to the next legal 16th-step. This inverts Beat Saber's 135deg-or-more rule (https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-mapping.html) for the correct reason, not by accident.
- RULE 1 — 'THE CUT IS THE MOVEMENT.' This is the flow engine and it makes choreography free. A cut leaves the thumb displaced in the direction of the cut, so the NEXT note belongs in the cell that displacement lands on. Therefore: a vertical-axis run walks the thumb between the two ROWS in a fixed lane (DOWN note sits in the top row and ends low; UP note sits in the bottom row and ends high), and a horizontal-axis run walks it between LANES in a fixed row (RIGHT note sits in the left/center lane, LEFT note sits in the center/right lane). Hard placement constraint that falls out of this and kills a whole class of bad notes: THE ARROW MUST ALWAYS POINT INTO THE GRID. Legal (cell, direction) pairs = UP only in the bottom row (3 cells), DOWN only in the top row (3), RIGHT only in center+left lanes (4), LEFT only in center+right lanes (4) = 14 directional combos + 6 free-note cells = 20-symbol vocabulary. The 10 banned combos are 'exit strokes' and are reserved by Rule 12. This is the direct port of BSMG's momentum/rotation flow ('rotating in same direction builds natural flow') and it also satisfies exceed7's ergonomics note that axis-aligned motion beats diagonal.
- RULE 2 — THE 8TH-NOTE LAW: EIGHTHS ARE ALWAYS REVERSALS. At 136 BPM the 8th note is 220.6ms, right at the human floor for a discrete deliberate gesture. Therefore any two notes 1/8 apart MUST be a 180-degree reversal on the same axis. Corollary: THE AXIS CAN ONLY CHANGE ON A GAP OF 3/16 (330.9ms) OR LONGER. This single rule converts every fast passage into a clean single-axis oscillation (a 'trill' in mania terms) and places every axis turn on a musical accent, which is exactly what makes hand-made charts feel choreographed and auto-maps feel like noise. It also means the generator never has to reason about diagonal thumb travel at speed.
- RULE 3 — TIER TABLE, SNAPPED TO YOUR 136 BPM GRID. Four tiers named for the chain (SEED / RELAY / DEEP / SEALED). Grid units: 1/16=110.3ms, 1/8=220.6ms, 3/16=330.9ms, 1/4=441.2ms, 3/8=661.8ms, 1/2=882.4ms, bar=1764.7ms. Minimum gaps by transition type: SEED — reversal 1/4 (441ms), 90deg turn 3/8 (662ms), jack 1/2 (882ms), max 1 jack per 8 bars. RELAY — reversal 3/16 (331ms), 90deg 1/4 (441ms), jack 1/2 (882ms). DEEP — reversal 1/8 (221ms) max 4 consecutive, 90deg 3/16 (331ms), jack 1/4 (441ms) max 2 consecutive. SEALED — reversal 1/8 max 12 consecutive with a mandatory >=1/4 rest after any run of 6+, 90deg 3/16 (never 1/8), jack 3/16 (331ms) max 3 consecutive. ABSOLUTE FLOORS at every tier: no two separate gestures closer than 200ms; never two notes at the same timestamp (one thumb means the chart is strictly MONOPHONIC — no chords, ever, which is the biggest single deviation from mania/Beat Saber and must be enforced as an invariant); chain links may be 1/16 apart because a chain is ONE gesture (Rule 8). Calibration check: SEALED at ~2.6 NPS sits inside BSMG's per-hand Expert band (1.21-3.91), SEED at ~0.85 NPS sits in the per-hand Normal band.
- RULE 4 — REARRANGE THE SONG SO THE LEVEL ENDS ON ITS PEAK, AND MAKE THE LAST BAR THE SEAL. public/chop/js/music.js currently runs INTRO 8 / BUILD 8 / DROP 16 / BREAK 8, which ends the 70.6s level on the lowest-energy section (sectionOf() clamps BREAK to intensity <=2). Change the 40-bar cycle to INTRO 8 / BUILD 8 / DROP 12 / BREAK 4 / FINAL 8, so bars 32-39 are the climax and bar 39 is the SEAL. The BREAK becomes a 4-bar hole that doubles as the mandatory rest (Rule 9) and as the tension-release before the payoff, which is exactly osu!'s 'show a gradual progression' plus the taiko rest requirement. The seal chop — one enormous emphasis note on bar 39 beat 1, sitting on the coinbase transaction — is the moment the block becomes a trophy, and it is the ONE unrepeatable emphasis in the level.
- RULE 5 — NOTES PER BAR PER SECTION PER TIER (the density arc, as integers a generator can hit exactly). Bar = 1.765s. Sections after Rule 4: INTRO 8 bars, BUILD 8, DROP 12, BREAK 4, FINAL 8. Notes/bar targets — SEED: 1 / 1 / 2 / 1 / 2 = 60 notes total (0.85 NPS). RELAY: 2 / 2 / 3 / 1 / 3 = 96 (1.36 NPS). DEEP: 2 / 3 / 4 / 2 / 5 = 136 (1.93 NPS). SEALED: 3 / 4 / 6 / 2 / 6 = 184 (2.61 NPS). Two hard constraints on the arc: (a) no adjacent 8-bar phrase may exceed 1.6x the previous phrase's NPS unless the music changes section — that is the 'density cliff' ban; (b) the FINAL section must be >= the DROP in notes/bar at every tier, so the level always ends at or above its previous peak. Note that notes/bar maps straight to subdivision: 2/bar = half notes, 4/bar = quarters, 8/bar = straight eighths (the ceiling).
- RULE 6 — VOICE-TO-CHART MAPPING, LOCKED FOR THE WHOLE LEVEL (the anti-random contract). One thumb means one note per onset, so you need a PRIORITY LADDER, not a layering stack. When voices collide on a 16th step, the highest-priority voice owns the note: snare > stab > kick > open/metal hat > bass > closed hat > ghost 16th. Then the fixed assignments, which must never vary within a level (this is osu!mania's 'do not use different kinds of patterns for exactly the same sound' and taiko's 'heavier/lower = don, lighter/higher = kat'): KICK (16ths 0/4/8/12, low, heavy) -> DOWN cut, top row, center-weighted, the anchor of the vertical oscillation. SNARE (16ths 4 and 12, the backbeat, higher) -> UP cut, bottom row, and it is always visually the bigger note. Because kick and snare alternate on the quarters, the SEED chart is literally DOWN-UP-DOWN-UP at 441ms — a perfect free-reversal chain that requires zero cleverness from the generator. CLOSED HAT (even 16ths) -> fills the current oscillation, inherits the current axis, small note. METAL/OPEN HAT (16ths 2/6/10/14) -> triggers a LANE STEP (move one lane in the direction of travel). STAB (build 6/14; drop 2/6/10/14) -> the AXIS FLIP trigger, and it is the only voice permitted to change axis. BASS (quarters) -> adds visual mass to whatever note it coincides with, and IS the chart on its own at SEED during the intro. PAD (16th 0) -> never a note; it is the bar-line visual only. GHOST 16ths (3/7/11/15) -> never standalone notes; they are chain links only, SEALED tier only. FILL (last bar of each section, 16ths 8-15) -> always a CHAIN, never 8 gestures.
- RULE 7 — TRAVEL COST: A LANE JUMP BUYS TIME, IT DOES NOT COME FREE. Port BSMG's spacing ladder (0 = inline/low effort, 1 = standard, 2 = jump/medium emphasis, 3+ = large emphasis needing real movement) plus its 'danger dash' ban. Manhattan distance between consecutive cells on the 3x2 grid: distance 0-1 = base snap, no penalty. Distance 2 = bump the required gap UP one grid step (1/8 becomes 3/16, 3/16 becomes 1/4). Distance 3 (opposite corners, e.g. bottom-left to top-right) = bump TWO steps, and BAN it outright at SEED and RELAY. Additionally, no more than 2 distance-2-or-greater moves per 8-bar phrase below SEALED — that is the 'excessive movement is emphasis, and emphasis is rationed' rule, and it stops the chart from turning into a thumb marathon.
- RULE 8 — CHAINS: ONE STROKE, MANY NOTES. This is how you honour the 16th-note fill and the drop's double-time hats without exceeding the thumb. A CHAIN is 2-5 cells in a straight line on ONE axis, adjacent, consumed by a single continuous stroke; links may sit 1/16 (110ms) apart because the gap rules count a chain as ONE gesture while the score counts it as N notes. Caps per 8-bar phrase: SEED 0, RELAY 1, DEEP 2, SEALED 3. Mandatory uses: every section's last-bar snare roll (16ths 8-15) is a chain; a CoinJoin / batch transaction with many outputs is a chain (see Rule 18). Entry rule: the chain's first link must be reachable under Rule 7 from the previous note, and the whole chain must fit inside one row (horizontal chain) or one lane (vertical chain). Grounded in Beat Saber arcs/chains: 'ease the swing requirements needed to fully score on connected blocks.'
- RULE 9 — MANDATORY REST, ENFORCED AS AN INVARIANT. Ported from osu!taiko (rest every 16-20 beats on Oni, 32-36 on lower, minimum length 1/1 to 3/1) and osu!standard ('a 1/1 or longer gap for every two measures'). CHOP: SEED — a rest of >= 2 bars in every 8 bars. RELAY — >= 1 bar in every 8. DEEP — >= 2 beats in every 8 bars, and >= 1 beat after any run of 4+ eighths. SEALED — >= 1 beat in every 4 bars, and a mandatory >= 1/4 rest after any 8th-run of 6 or more. Plus, at every tier, the 4-bar BREAK section is at most 1 note per bar and the bar immediately before the FINAL drop is SILENT — a full 1765ms of nothing under the riser. That silent bar is the single highest-value bar in the level: it is the telegraph, the breath, and the thing that makes the FINAL section land.
- RULE 10 — FREE-NOTE FRACTION, AND THE RULE THAT KEEPS THEM HONEST. Free (any-direction) notes are Beat Saber's dot notes and they exist to reduce load and to open new motions, NOT to escape hard transitions. Fractions: SEED 40%, RELAY 22%, DEEP 12%, SEALED 6%. THE CRITICAL CONSTRAINT: free notes must be allocated BEFORE direction assignment and must be tied to a specific musical voice (recommendation: the open hat at 16th 14, plus the first note after any rest — the 're-entry' note, which is where a directional demand is least fair). A generator that emits a free note whenever its parity/gap check fails will produce exactly the automapper feel the community complains about, and it will be visible in the data as free notes clustering at hard transitions. Test invariant: the distribution of free notes across 16th-step positions must match the allocated voice's distribution within 10%, i.e. free notes must not correlate with constraint violations.
- RULE 11 — MOTIF: THE LEVEL IS BUILT FROM LICKS, NEVER FROM PER-ONSET DECISIONS. Author a library of 12-20 two-bar LICKS per tier. A lick is a sequence of (16th-step offset, lane, row, direction) tuples that is already legal under Rules 0-2 and 7. The generator assembles the 40 bars from licks, not from independent decisions — this is the direct answer to Dance Dance Convolution's finding that step selection needs pattern memory. Repetition schedule per 8-bar phrase: bars 1-2 lick A, bars 3-4 lick A VERBATIM (the second occurrence of the same musical figure gets the identical chart figure — osu!'s consistency law), bars 5-6 lick B, bars 7-8 lick A' (a ONE-AXIS variation of A). Legal variations, one at a time, never combined: (a) MIRROR — reflect lanes left<->right and swap LEFT<->RIGHT directions, keep UP/DOWN (the sanctioned variation per exceed7: 'mirror patterns work when physical execution remains comparable'); (b) ROW SHIFT — move the whole figure between rows; (c) TAIL SWAP — change only the final note's direction/cell. Level-wide: the DROP's lick set must reappear in the FINAL section — the finale should be the drop's licks at higher density, so the player RECOGNISES the climax rather than sight-reading it. exceed7 is explicit that the chorus should be memorable and consistent, with the technical material in transitions.
- RULE 12 — EMPHASIS BUDGET (rationed, per BSMG's 'if everything is emphasized, nothing is emphasized'). Three tiers of accent, with hard per-phrase caps. LIGHT accent (bigger note, brighter kerf, no mechanical change) — max 4 per 8-bar phrase, always on the snare. MEDIUM accent (a distance-2 lane jump, or a chain) — max 1 per phrase at SEED/RELAY, max 2 at DEEP/SEALED. HEAVY accent (an EXIT STROKE: the 10 combos Rule 1 bans, i.e. UP from the top row, DOWN from the bottom row, LEFT from the left lane, RIGHT from the right lane — the slash that flies off the play area) — max 1 per phrase, only on a section boundary, and never inside an 8th-run (taiko: finishers 'must not be used in any 1/4 patterns or faster'). And exactly ONE level-defining emphasis: the SEAL on bar 39, which uses a mechanic no other note in the level uses. Nothing else may look like the seal.
- RULE 13 — READABILITY: APPROACH TIME, ON-SCREEN BUDGET, AND THE OCCLUSION BAN. Approach (reaction) time per tier, scaled from BSMG's 500-600ms Expert+ band and pushed longer because phone arrow glyphs are small and the audience is 30-50: SEED 950ms, RELAY 850ms, DEEP 750ms, SEALED 650ms. Never below 550ms on a phone. Additionally, per BSMG's 'large movements warrant longer reaction time,' add +120ms of approach for any note that follows a distance-2+ travel. The arrow/kerf must be at full size and opacity for the last 400ms minimum. ON-SCREEN BUDGET (BSMG downmapping: 2-3 unique patterns per half-jump interval at Easy, 4-5 at Normal): SEED max 3 notes on screen at once, RELAY 4, DEEP 6, SEALED 8. OCCLUSION BAN (the face-note port): no two notes in the SAME LANE but DIFFERENT ROWS may be within 250ms of each other — in a perspective corridor the nearer one hides the farther one. Same lane + same row is fine (they read as sequential depth). THUMB OCCLUSION: the chop plane must sit at 60-68% of screen height with the bottom row ABOVE it, so the player's own thumb never covers an incoming note; cells must be at least 44x44pt (Apple HIG floor) and in practice ~110px lane pitch / ~90px row pitch on a 390x844 viewport.
- RULE 14 — THE FORBIDDEN-PATTERN LIST (build these as generator asserts, not as review notes). (1) SIMULTANEITY — two notes on the same 16th step. One thumb, monophonic, no exceptions. (2) SUB-FLOOR GAP — any two gestures under 200ms, or under the tier's transition-specific floor from Rule 3. (3) JACK SPAM — more than 2 consecutive same-direction notes below SEALED, more than 3 ever. (4) 90deg AT EIGHTHS — an axis change on a 1/8 gap (Rule 2). (5) CORNER DASH — Manhattan distance 3 at SEED/RELAY, or distance 3 in under 400ms at any tier (the 'danger dash' port). (6) OCCLUSION STACK — same lane, different row, within 250ms. (7) OUTWARD KERF — a directional note pointing out of the grid outside the Rule 12 emphasis budget (the 'visual clutter' / 'hammer hit' port: an arrow with no plausible motion behind it). (8) ORPHAN SIXTEENTH — a 16th-spaced note that is not part of a chain, ever (the 'norinori-ness' trap: isolated 16ths feel awkward because the listener's brain has already filled that space). (9) UNSIGNALLED RESET — a jack that does not get its full 2.0x gap. (10) ANCHOR — more than 4 consecutive notes in the same lane. (11) DENSITY CLIFF — a phrase exceeding 1.6x the previous phrase's NPS without a music-section change. (12) VOICE DRIFT — the same drum voice mapped to two different cut directions anywhere in one level. (13) EMPHASIS INFLATION — exceeding any Rule 12 cap. (14) MONOTONE RUN — more than 8 notes of unbroken single-axis oscillation without an axis change or lane step.
- RULE 15 — TIERED DIRECTION ENFORCEMENT (this is how you keep '4 directions' as a design language without punishing new players). Project Sekai's flicks are up-only and still award a GREAT on a wrong-direction upward swipe; BanG Dream accepts any direction. CHOP should draw all four directions always, but enforce them progressively: SEED — AXIS ONLY (a vertical kerf accepts up OR down; the polarity affects only the 15-point accuracy component, never the combo). RELAY — axis mandatory, wrong polarity costs the 15 points and the note scores 100/115 but KEEPS COMBO. DEEP — wrong polarity scores 70/115, keeps combo. SEALED — wrong polarity is a miss. This gives you a real skill ladder inside one mechanic, makes the tutorial unnecessary (the game teaches direction by paying for it), and means a first-time player physically cannot fail on direction — which is what killed v1 ('too hard').
- RULE 16 — THE FIRST 8 BARS ARE A TUTORIAL THAT NEVER SAYS SO. The INTRO must introduce the vocabulary one element at a time, per BSMG ('introduce unconventional patterns clearly at map start... establish movement expectations early') and exceed7 ('start from closely relatable double notes instead of starting from hard to read patterns'). Bars 1-2: vertical axis only, center lane only, quarter notes (DOWN-UP-DOWN-UP on kick/snare). Bars 3-4: same, plus one lane step so the player learns that lanes exist. Bars 5-6: introduce the horizontal axis with one L-R reversal per bar. Bars 7-8: first free note and first medium accent. Nothing new is introduced after bar 8 except the chain (introduced once, alone, in the BUILD's last-bar fill) and the seal (bar 39). Everything after bar 8 is recombination — which is precisely what makes a 70-second level feel composed rather than 'stale after seconds.'
- RULE 17 — TIMING WINDOWS MUST FIT INSIDE THE GAP FLOOR. Judgement windows have to be narrower than half the minimum gap or a single stroke can satisfy two notes and the chart becomes ambiguous. With a 200ms absolute gap floor and a 220.6ms 8th note: PERFECT +/-40ms, GREAT +/-70ms, GOOD +/-100ms, MISS beyond 100ms. Those windows sum to a 200ms envelope which exactly fits the 8th-note grid with no overlap. Map them to the 115: PERFECT with full stroke length and follow-through = 115; the 70-point component requires the swipe to travel >= 40 CSS px before crossing the note plane; the 30-point component requires >= 25px past it; the 15-point component is how close the stroke's line passes to the cell centre (full 15 within 20% of the cell half-width). Chart consequence: the generator must guarantee stroke room, so the required gap after a note is measured from the note's FOLLOW-THROUGH END, not from the note time — add 60ms to every gap computation as the follow-through reservation. 87% of the score lives outside the hit instant (https://bsmg.wiki/ranking-guide.html); a chart that does not budget for it feels unfair no matter how good the timing code is.
- RULE 18 — MAKE THE BLOCK CONNECTION MECHANICAL, NOT DECORATIVE (this is the v2 'no explanation of the block connection' fix). Every note IS a transaction, and the mapping must be visible without a tutorial: NOTE COUNT = a deterministic function of the block's transaction count (bucketed, so the chart length stays within the Rule 5 targets — e.g. tier auto-selects so notes ~= clamp(txCount/25, 60, 184), and the HUD literally reads '3,412 transactions -> 136 chops'). NOTE SIZE = transaction vsize (a 200-vbyte payment is a small block, a 40-input consolidation is a slab). ROW = fee-rate band — high-fee transactions ride the TOP row ('priority lane'), low-fee the bottom; this makes row placement mean something and gives you a free bitcoin lesson. CHAIN = a batch/CoinJoin transaction with many outputs: one sweep through many cells, which is exactly what a batch is. THE SEAL = the coinbase transaction, bar 39, the miner's reward, the moment the block is yours. Block hash drives only PRESENTATION and LIBRARY SELECTION — see Rule 19.
- RULE 19 — THE BLOCK HASH MAY PICK, BUT MAY NEVER PLACE. The hash selects which licks come out of the library, which phrase template is used, the mirror/row-shift variation schedule, the BPM within a narrow band, and the palette. It must NEVER decide an individual note's direction, lane, or timing. This is the single most important anti-'random feel' rule and it is why hand-authored charts beat automappers: the randomness lives at the level of MUSICAL CHOICES, not at the level of individual notes. It also makes the chart provably legal by construction (every lick in the library is pre-validated), which means your replay-verification story is trivial — the server re-derives the same chart from the same block height and the same library version.
- RULE 20 — SHIP THE RULE SET AS A TEST SUITE, THE WAY EVERY OTHER GALAXY MIND ENGINE IS SHIPPED. Put the generator in public/chop/js/chart.js as a PURE module (no rAF, no Date.now, no AudioContext — same discipline as lib/hodl-trail.ts and lib/wen.ts) that takes {blockHeight, txSummary, tier, libraryVersion} and returns a note list in integer 16th-steps. Then add <repo>/scripts/test-chop-chart.ts to `npm run verify` asserting, over ~500 synthetic blocks x 4 tiers: (1) monophonic — no two notes share a 16th step; (2) every gap >= the Rule 3 tier floor for its transition type, with the Rule 7 travel bump and the Rule 17 follow-through reservation applied; (3) no forbidden pattern from Rule 14 occurs, all 14 checked by name; (4) notes-per-section within +/-10% of the Rule 5 targets; (5) FINAL section notes/bar >= DROP notes/bar; (6) every legality check passes WITHOUT the repair pass having converted anything to a free note (assert free-note count == allocated count); (7) rest requirements from Rule 9 satisfied; (8) emphasis caps from Rule 12 satisfied; (9) every directional note points into the grid except budgeted exit strokes; (10) determinism — same input, byte-identical chart; (11) every drum voice maps to exactly one cut direction across the whole level; (12) each lick's second occurrence is byte-identical to its first; (13) termination — the constraint solver always produces a legal chart for any input within N iterations. Invariants 6 and 13 are the ones that actually protect chart FEEL; the rest protect correctness.
- RULE 21 — GENERATOR PIPELINE, IN ORDER (each stage is separately testable, which is how you avoid the Beat Sage failure mode). (1) SELECT — walk music.js's 16th grid, apply the Rule 6 priority ladder, and keep onsets until the Rule 5 notes/bar budget for this section and tier is met (drop lowest-priority voices first — this is 'layering' from mania charting and it is what makes low tiers still feel musical rather than merely sparse). (2) SEGMENT — cut into 8-bar phrases, assign the Rule 11 lick schedule. (3) PLACE — walk the grid with 'the cut is the movement' (Rule 1), axis changes only on 3/16+ gaps (Rule 2). (4) REPAIR — a bounded solver that fixes any Rule 14 violation by DELETING a note or WIDENING a gap, never by converting to a free note. (5) EMPHASIZE — spend the Rule 12 budget on the strongest onsets in each phrase. (6) REST — enforce Rule 9, deleting notes if needed. (7) CHAIN — convert the fill bars and batch transactions per Rule 8. (8) VERIFY — run the Rule 20 assertions at runtime in dev; in prod, fall back to a known-good static chart if verification fails, so a weird block can never ship a broken level.
- RULE 22 — THE 'DOES IT FEEL AMAZING' ACCEPTANCE TEST, since none of the above proves fun. Three checks before you call the chart good. (a) THE HUM TEST: play the chart with the audio muted and tap along to the note stream; if a listener who knows the song can identify which section you are in from the note rhythm alone, the chart represents the music. If it cannot, you are charting onsets rather than the song. (b) THE SECOND-PLAY TEST: on run 2, a player should ANTICIPATE the FINAL section's licks because they heard them in the DROP. If run 2 feels like sight-reading again, Rule 11's motif schedule is not doing its job — that is the direct fix for 'stale after seconds,' because the pleasure in run 2 is recognition, not novelty. (c) THE THUMB TEST: play the SEALED chart three times back to back on a phone. If your thumb aches, Rules 7/9/12 are being violated somewhere — BSMG names shoulder strain from 'sky streams' as a real mapping defect, and a mobile audience of 30-50 year old men will simply stop playing rather than complain.
- RULE 23 — GRADE THRESHOLDS SHOULD MAKE THE FIRST BLOCK A WIN. Since 87% of a note's score is stroke quality, a competent-but-imprecise player naturally scores ~85-90% of max even with perfect timing. Set letter grades on percentage of theoretical max (notes x 115 x multiplier): S 96%+, A 90%, B 80%, C 68%, D 50%, and SEAL THE BLOCK at C or better. That means a first-timer on SEED almost always seals their first block and joins THE CHAIN on run one, which is the retention moment; S remains genuinely hard because it demands full pre-swing and follow-through on nearly every note. This is the direct fix for v1's 'too hard' — difficulty lives in the grade, not in the seal.
- RULE 24 — WHAT TO EXPLICITLY NOT BUILD, WITH REASONS. No chords/simultaneous notes (one thumb). No 16th-note gestures outside chains (below the human floor at 136 BPM — 110ms). No hold/sustain notes in v1 (a held thumb blocks the next gesture entirely and eats the screen; Beat Saber's own guidance warns of 'long note syndrome'). No bombs/obstacles in v1 (BSMG's own forbidden list is mostly bomb-related, and a mobile player who fails on an avoid-mechanic quits). No diagonal cut directions (8 directions doubles the readability load on a 6-inch screen for no musical gain — 4 directions x 6 cells is already 20 legal symbols). No variable scroll speed / BPM gimmicks (exceed7's 'slowdown syndrome' warning; also they break the fixed 16th-step integer grid that makes the whole rule set testable).

# CURVE

Deep research on difficulty, onboarding and the skill ladder for CHOP, read against 25+ sources (Beat Saber's official mapping tables, Beatstar, Guitar Hero, Rhythm Heaven, ADOFAI, Hades, Celeste, Cuphead, RE4, Oblivion, Mario Kart, Fruit Ninja, Rhythm Quest devlogs, osu! progression threads, GameAnalytics 2026 benchmarks, the Wilson 85% learning paper, and mobile-puzzle difficulty-modelling literature).

The single most important number found: fewer than 7% of mobile users retry after two consecutive failures inside their first five minutes. That one stat determines the whole architecture — CHOP must be structurally incapable of failing a first-timer twice, which means no-fail is not a setting, it is the shape of the first three blocks.

The second most important finding is a design split that almost every failed DDA implementation gets wrong: difficulty must be TWO layers. A visible layer (note density, lanes, cut axes) that the player earns, sees, and which is NEVER auto-adjusted — because the moment the game silently moves the yardstick the player measures themselves against, you get the Oblivion level-scaling failure, which is precisely the "no goal, stale after seconds" complaint that killed v2. And an invisible layer (contact windows, angle tolerance, approach margin) that adapts silently, is capped, is never named, and never touches score — the coyote-time / Half-Life / RE4 model, the only kind of assist players never resent.

Concrete headline numbers, computed against CHOP's actual 136 BPM / ~70s / 3-lane x 2-row spec: run 1 should be 1.10 NPS (77 notes, one note per two beats, 1.40s approach) — that is Beat Saber Easy's floor, which is correct for a normie's thumb. Run 20 (block 10) should be 4.88 NPS (342 notes, 0.75s approach), which is the human ceiling for a single-thumb swipe verb and matches Rhythm Quest's measured 4.8 presses/sec cap. The ramp between rungs is x1.18 per block — the inverse of Beat Saber's official 20%-per-tier downmapping constant. Target first-run block-seal rate: 100% (structurally). Target first-run note hit-rate: 88-92% median. Target grade distribution on run 1: ~68% B or better, ~10% A, under 2% S. Target second-run start rate within the same session: 55%+ — that, not D1, is the metric that tells you whether CHOP is addictive.

On skill expression: the research converges on one answer — a beginner cannot see accuracy, but they can HEAR it. Fruit Ninja's blitz tiers escalate the drum roll so a bystander hears skill; Guitar Hero's Star Power turns the screen purple and the crowd roars. CHOP's combo x1-x8 should gate instrument layers in music.js, and should bank a player-deployed 2x window (a Star Power analog) so an expert can score 3x a beginner on the identical daily chart without the chart ever changing. That is what makes a shared daily leaderboard meaningful.

## Findings

- THE LOAD-BEARING RETENTION STAT: fewer than 7% of users retry an experience after two consecutive failures within their initial five minutes; only 18% of users continue with an app that presents a steep learning curve in the first session, versus 41% when newcomers get gradual onboarding with immediate feedback and manageable objectives; and offering several adjustable challenge modes produced a 22% increase in 30-day loyalty (GameAnalytics 2024 data, summarised at https://moldstud.com/articles/p-the-impact-of-game-difficulty-on-mobile-game-retention-rates-strategies-for-success). Read together: a first-timer who fails twice in five minutes is gone, and there is no recovery mechanic that beats simply not failing them.
- BEAT SABER'S OFFICIAL NPS BANDS (the only published, authoritative note-density table in the genre, computed across all base-game maps — https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/basic-mapping.html): Easy 0.82-2.29 NPS, Normal 1.05-3.41, Hard 1.73-5.22, Expert 2.42-7.81, Expert+ 3.21-10.66. Paired NJS/Jump-Distance: Easy 10-12 NJS / 25-30 JD, Normal 10-12 / 25-30, Hard 10-15 / 23-30, Expert 12-18 / 21-27, Expert+ 15-23 / 19-27. The downmapping guide (https://bsmg.wiki/mapping/downmapping.html) states the ramp constant explicitly: reduce overall note density by approximately 20% per step as you work DOWN the spread — i.e. x1.25 up, or x1.18-1.20 for a finer 10-rung ladder.
- REACTION-TIME MATH CONVERTED TO CHOP'S 136 BPM (beat period 441ms; a 70s level = 158.7 beats = 39.7 bars of 4/4). Using Beat Saber's reaction-time formula (JD = NJS x (60/BPM) x HJD x 2) against its own published NJS/JD bands, approach times at 136 BPM are: Easy 1.375s, Normal 1.23s, Hard 1.00s, Expert 0.80s, Expert+ 0.55s. Beat Saber's own worked example confirms the formula: 120 BPM at NJS 16 gives a 2-beat half-jump = 750ms of reaction time. CHOP is one thumb on a phone with finger occlusion, not two hands in VR, so 0.55s is below CHOP's floor — 0.70-0.75s is the practical hard floor.
- THE 85% RULE FOR OPTIMAL LEARNING (Wilson, Shenhav, Straccia & Cohen, Nature Communications 2019, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12552-4): for all stochastic-gradient-descent-based learners — which includes the biologically plausible networks used to model animal and human skill acquisition — the optimal training error rate is 15.87%, i.e. ~85% success. The paper's own framing is a 'zone of proximal difficulty'. This is the correct target for CHOP runs 2 onward. It is deliberately NOT the correct target for run 1, where the job is a first impression rather than a learning rate.
- MATURE-DIFFICULTY EQUILIBRIUM: empirical study of Lily's Garden telemetry (https://arxiv.org/html/2401.17436v1) found difficulty stabilises at ~3.2 attempts per level completion once past the 100 tutorial levels, with easy levels cleared in one attempt and hard levels averaging 7+, and notes that average-attempts-per-completion is the metric directly linked to churn. Separately, a healthy first-session completion rate is ~70%, and below 30% means the opening needs reconstruction rather than tuning (https://recognizingpatterns.substack.com/p/first-time-user-experience-your-player). Note the asymmetry: the game may reach 3.2 attempts eventually, but must be at ~1.0 attempts on day one.
- 2026 RETENTION BENCHMARKS (GameAnalytics, 16,262 mobile games with 1,000+ MAU, via https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game): D1 median ~20%, top-25% ~30%, top-10% ~40%, top-1% 64-68%. D7 median under 4%, top-10% 11-12%. D30 median 0.69-0.79%. Session length top-25% ~5.2 minutes; daily playtime top-25% 22-24 min. Arcade games specifically LEAD all genres on D1 but collapse on long-term retention. D1 declined industry-wide in 2025 vs 2024. Implication for CHOP: a ~5 minute session at 70s per block means the target session is 4-5 blocks, and the arcade genre's known weakness — D30 — is exactly what THE CHAIN meta-progression exists to fix.
- HOW RHYTHM HEAVEN TEACHES WITHOUT A TUTORIAL: it uses visual and auditory systems hidden in plain sight to teach the rules, and specifically PATTERN MATCHING — non-player characters act FIRST, and the player imitates the pattern they just watched (https://medium.com/quick-game-design-notes/rhythm-heaven-quick-design-notes-5abb6ce52e97). The emphasis is on internalising the pulse and trusting the ears rather than reacting to visual prompts. Its Practice sections precede any game introducing a new mechanic, are diegetic rather than menu-based, and are SKIPPABLE via Select (https://rhwiki.net/wiki/Practice). A Dance of Fire and Ice uses the same architecture at level scale: 20 worlds, each introducing one new shape/rhythm via short tutorial levels followed by a full-length boss level — 95% positive across 15,000+ reviews.
- FIRST-SESSION TIMING WINDOWS: players decide whether a mobile game is worth keeping in about 7 seconds, and the stay-or-churn decision lands within the first 30-60 seconds. Pokemon Go's canonical example: new players caught their first Pokemon within 60 seconds of opening the app, with no instruction manual — it showed rather than told. The recommended tutorial structure is teach-test-twist (introduce, then require, then combine), and human working memory caps at three to seven things at once (https://recognizingpatterns.substack.com/p/first-time-user-experience-your-player, https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/first-time-user-experience).
- BEATSTAR'S KEY STRUCTURAL CHOICE — no per-song difficulty label: 'unlike most rhythm games you're familiar with, songs don't have a general difficulty to them. Instead, as you progress deeper into a song, the level rises and the tap chart becomes increasingly difficult' (https://beatstar.fandom.com/wiki/Difficulties). Grading is medal-based, not pass/fail: 5 stars and four Mastery Medals (Gold, Platinum, Diamond, Diamond Perfect). Judgement is Perfect+/Perfect/Great, and critically the game 'only punishes less-than-perfect hits' by ending the streak — the multiplier drops one level per break rather than resetting to zero. Beatstar reached 35M+ players and ~$65M in nine months. Its documented weakness is directly relevant: Naavik attributes its IAP revenue decline to 'lack of variation in terms of systems and core gameplay' (https://naavik.co/deep-dives/beatstar-west-finds-rhythm/) — the same staleness complaint that killed CHOP v2.
- GUITAR HERO REDUCES VOCABULARY, NOT SPEED: Easy uses only the first three frets (Green/Red/Yellow), Medium adds a fourth, Hard adds the orange fret, Expert plays every audible note (https://guitarhero.fandom.com/wiki/Easy). The lowest tier is explicitly justified as teaching players 'how to shift to different frets to play notes in different lanes' — i.e. the beginner tier exists to teach the SPATIAL verb, not to slow the music down. Beat Saber's downmapping guide does the same thing rhythmically: Hard simplifies 1/4 timings out of Expert, Normal simplifies 1/2 out of Hard, Easy simplifies further 1/2 and 1/1 out of Normal.
- BEAT SABER'S NO-FAIL IS THE BEST-DESIGNED ASSIST IN THE GENRE: after a 2021 revision, No Fail can be left permanently on and costs nothing until it is actually used — the score is only multiplied by 0.5 once Energy has fully depleted, at which point the player continues rather than being ejected (https://x.com/BeatSaber/status/1357406655055605760). The design intent was to allow those scores onto leaderboards without spawning a thousand separate boards per modifier configuration. Rock Band took the purer route: no-fail as a plain party-friendly toggle with no score penalty at all (https://www.engadget.com/2008-07-16-rock-band-2-features-no-fail-option.html). Both share the key property: the assist never gates content.
- HADES GOD MODE — THE FAILURE-TRIGGERED RAMP: 20% damage resistance to start, +2% each time a run ends in death, capping at 80% (https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/God_Mode). The design property that makes it work: it never permanently removes challenge, because as soon as it has helped you complete a run, the difficulty steps back up. It ramps ONLY on failure and never on success, which means it cannot punish a player for improving. Supergiant's stated secondary motive was development efficiency — one scalar beats balancing multiple discrete difficulties across every weapon-and-boon permutation.
- CELESTE'S ASSIST MODE AND THE PATRONISING TRAP: the original preamble said difficulty was 'essential' to the experience and recommended playing without assists first. This was widely read as implying assist-mode players weren't really playing the game, and the developers changed the wording from 'essential' to 'intended' after player collaboration (https://www.vice.com/en/article/celeste-assist-mode-change-and-accessibility/). The other structural lesson: the options are GRANULAR (game speed as a percentage, infinite stamina, extra air dashes, invincibility, chapter skip) rather than a single Easy switch, because different players need different kinds of help. Related genre norm: the lowest tier is routinely renamed to de-stigmatise it — 'Storyteller', 'Narrative', 'Story Time' — because players resist selecting something labelled Easy (https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/StoryDifficultySetting).
- CUPHEAD IS THE CANONICAL BOTCHED GATE: Simple mode does not merely lower difficulty, it CUTS boss phases and withholds Soul Contracts, so a player who beats World One on Simple cannot progress to World Two and must replay everything on Regular (https://www.kitguru.net/gaming/damien-cox/cupheads-simple-mode-cuts-content/). The sharpest critique: 'not a single phase deleted from simple mode couldn't have had the challenge scaled back instead' — slower projectiles, more telegraphed attacks, fewer hit points. Rule extracted: an assist may change how hard content is, never whether you may have it.
- OBLIVION LEVEL SCALING IS THE CANONICAL BOTCHED DDA: enemies level alongside the player, so 'the disparity between players and enemies diminishes' and level-ups become moot — bandits in glass armour at high level break the feeling of having become powerful (https://gamerant.com/oblivion-remastered-level-scaling-progression-bad-good/). This is exactly the failure mode a game whose stated goal is 'make the player feel themselves improving' must avoid. Mario Kart's rubber banding is the short-horizon twin: it reads as unfair because 'the computer doesn't have to follow the same rules' (https://remptongames.medium.com/more-than-meets-the-eye-the-secrets-of-dynamic-difficulty-adjustment-bd35d6a08c82).
- DDA DONE WELL IS INVISIBLE, AND THE BEST KIND IS 'ALWAYS ON' RATHER THAN ADAPTIVE. Resident Evil 4 maintains a hidden difficulty value fed by death frequency, accuracy, damage taken and time-in-area, driving enemy aggression, damage, drop rates and spawn counts — and is praised specifically because players don't notice it (https://www.resetera.com/threads/i-just-found-out-re4-had-dynamic-difficulty-how-many-games-have-it-without-it-being-obvious.153006/). Rempton's taxonomy classes a further category as permanently-active hidden generosity: Devil May Cry slows or stops off-screen enemies; Half-Life caps simultaneous attackers at two while the rest flank; platformers extend the jump window after leaving a ledge. God Hand is the noted counter-example — a fully VISIBLE adaptive meter that also works, because constant information beats suspicion. The derived rule everywhere: 'the player should never feel it happening', and players perceive environments as rigged when hidden adjustment is suspected.
- COYOTE TIME AND INPUT BUFFERING — THE MECHANISM BEHIND 'FEELS TIGHT': the human eye processes visual information approximately 13ms behind reality, so by the time a player SEES their character leave a ledge it has already been airborne for several frames; coyote time forgives late input, input buffering forgives early input, and neither is visible (https://www.gamejuice.co.uk/articles/coyote-time-input-buffering). Celeste and Super Meat Boy are cited as the best-feeling platformer controls ever made and neither community discusses these as features — they say the controls feel 'tight'. The article's calibration test for whether forgiveness is tuned right is a player saying 'this jump finally feels like my fault when I miss' — forgiveness that does its work without stealing accountability.
- TOUCH-INPUT PHYSICAL LIMITS: users cannot perceive tap-response latency below 24ms, but CAN detect drag latency as low as 6ms with drag performance degrading above 25ms — i.e. a swipe verb is far more latency-sensitive than a tap verb, which matters because CHOP's verb is a swipe. Arcade rhythm games grant at most 50ms of leniency for the top judgement, and practical timing windows range 10-100ms depending on hardware (https://us.ktcplay.com/blogs/support-tips/refresh-rate-for-rhythm-games, patent literature on touch timing). Rhythm-game engineering consensus is that the touch TIMESTAMP, not the frame time, is the correct clock for judgement.
- FRUIT NINJA MAKES SKILL AUDIBLE — the single cheapest skill-expression mechanic found in this research. Combos of 8+ fruit trigger 'combo blitz'; continuing to combo raises the Blitz TIER, and 'the drums get louder each time the Blitz tier increases', with the sixth and maximum tier ('Unbelievable Blitz') modulating the drum roll into a more intense version while fruit spawn at their most alarming rate (https://fruitninja.fandom.com/wiki/Blitz). Halfbrick explicitly cites Street Fighter / King of Fighters combo strings as the inspiration. A bystander who cannot read a score can HEAR that someone is playing well.
- COMBO IS THE LEGIBLE AXIS, ACCURACY IS THE CONNOISSEUR AXIS: in Guitar Hero and osu!standard-style scoring, a player with 90% accuracy and a Full Combo can outscore a player with 99% accuracy who broke combo mid-song, because Full Combo guarantees maximum time at the highest multiplier — which is why FC is the coveted, communicable achievement (https://rhythm-games.com/guides/rhythm-game-scoring-system-explained). Guitar Hero's Star Power is the deployment-choice layer on top: banked by hitting a marked phrase perfectly, spent at the player's discretion, and the community built entire wikis (ScoreHero) around optimal SP PATHS — an expert scoring layer that requires zero change to the note chart.
- THE HUMAN CEILING FOR A ONE-VERB RHYTHM GAME IS ~4.8 ACTIONS/SEC: Rhythm Quest (DDRKirby(ISQ), a career rhythm-game developer) implemented a non-linear difficulty curve mapping actions-per-second to a 1-100 difficulty scale, 'currently capping out at around 4.8 presses per second'. His closed-alpha feedback is also instructive: testers rated difficulty 'either just right or a little too hard', and the two biggest onboarding failures were LATENCY CALIBRATION being 'very opaque' to first-time users (players cannot verify a 30ms offset visually) and visual READABILITY of action sequences — he cites A Dance of Fire and Ice's relative-visual-reference calibration as the model (https://ddrkirbyisq.medium.com/rhythm-quest-devlog-14-closed-alpha-test-feedback-5df7e1e46347).
- SCROLL SPEED IS NOT DIFFICULTY — a settled position in the rhythm community, and the cleanest route to non-patronising options: approach-speed adjustment exists 'for people who want to be more comfortable at reading notes, but not reducing the difficulty'; if you want an easier chart you change the DIFFICULTY, not the speed. Absence of offset and scroll-speed settings is described as a 'cardinal sin' in the genre (https://steamcommunity.com/app/980610/discussions/0/2912094978031450682/). Because faster is also a legitimate preference, exposing the control reads as customisation rather than pity.
- HOW LONG SKILL ACTUALLY TAKES (osu! community consensus on star-rating progression): 1-2 stars ~1 week, 2-3 stars ~1 month, 3-4 stars several months; the 3-to-4 star jump is described as the hardest, and 'many players give up at 3 stars because it takes such a long time to progress out of that range' (https://osu.ppy.sh/community/forums/topics/1497756, https://osu.ppy.sh/community/forums/topics/1592297). The design lesson: perceived plateaus kill players, so any ladder needs sub-rung feedback that fires far more often than a rung promotion does.
- MENU DIFFICULTY SELECTION IS ITSELF A DESIGN FAILURE for a first-timer: 'explicitly selecting a difficulty mode in a menu-based manner is not an interesting choice, and generally speaking, players are not going to be good at weighting short-term convenience against long-term enjoyment because they just do not know the game enough' — the argument for Organic Difficulty, where players set challenge through in-game choices rather than a pre-play menu (https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/a-different-approach-to-difficulty). The counterweight from the same literature: 'neither group is punished for playing the game their way; expert players don't have to unlock the higher settings and newcomers can enjoy the base experience' — so once a player HAS information, free selection must be available.
- LEFT 4 DEAD'S AI DIRECTOR PROVES REST IS A DESIGN MATERIAL, NOT DEAD TIME: it cycles Build Up -> Peak (infected largely stop spawning at maximum intensity) -> Relax (no wanderers, mobs or specials spawn at all), and drives mood with dynamic music and visual cues (https://left4dead.fandom.com/wiki/The_Director). A peak only reads as a peak if a trough precedes it — directly applicable to shaping a 70-second level's internal curve rather than running a flat density.

## Recommendations

- 1. MAKE FAILURE STRUCTURALLY IMPOSSIBLE FOR THE FIRST THREE BLOCKS, AND SHOW NO DIFFICULTY MENU BEFORE RUN 1. This is the highest-impact change available and it is nearly free. Under 7% of users retry after two consecutive failures in the first five minutes, so a fail state in run 1 is not a difficulty problem, it is a funnel amputation. Blocks 1-3 have NO fail condition at all: a miss costs combo, never life, and the block always seals — the grade tells the truth instead. main.js already carries this intent in its header comment ('nothing instant-fails, misses cost combo not life') — keep it and make it architectural rather than a tuning choice. From block 4 onward introduce a soft floor only: dropping below 40% accuracy over a rolling 8-bar window triggers a 4-bar 'MEMPOOL THINS' passage at 0.4x density (Left 4 Dead's Relax phase), never a game-over. And show no tier menu at all until block 3 clears — menu difficulty selection is an uninformed, uninteresting choice for someone who has never seen the game, and the audience here is a normie friend opening a link on a phone.
- 2. THE FIRST 70-SECOND BLOCK IS THE TUTORIAL. NO MODAL, NO TEXT WALL, NO SKIP BUTTON. Time-to-first-slash must be under 4 seconds from the boot tap; players decide in ~7 seconds and stay-or-churn inside 30-60s. Exact bar plan at 136 BPM (441ms/beat, 39.7 bars in 70s), using Rhythm Heaven's imitation model plus teach-test-twist: BARS 1-2 — one target, centre lane, bottom row, horizontal kerf, arriving on beat 1 of bar 2; the rest of the scene dims to ~25% and a GHOST BLADE traces the required stroke one beat before arrival (NPCs act first, the player imitates). BARS 3-4 — three more singles, centre lane only, ghost blade fades out after the second. BARS 5-8 — lanes widen to all 3 (bottom row only), still horizontal only, one note per bar. BAR 9 — first VERTICAL kerf, ghost-demonstrated exactly once. BAR 12 — first x2 combo lands, and the music adds its second instrument layer on that beat, so the reward for the thing just learned is audible. BARS 13-40 — full block-1 chart at 1.10 NPS. That is ~10.5 seconds of hand-holding buried inside a real level with nothing to skip and nothing to read. Teach exactly ONE new thing per block thereafter (working memory caps at 3-7 items): B2 vertical, B3 top row, B4 the red tx (bomb), B5 diagonals, B6 doubles, B7 the long chop. Guitar Hero's model — Easy is three of five frets — says restrict the SPATIAL VOCABULARY first, not the tempo.
- 3. SPLIT DIFFICULTY INTO A VISIBLE EARNED LAYER AND AN INVISIBLE ADAPTIVE LAYER, AND NEVER LET THE ADAPTIVE ONE TOUCH THE SCORE. This is the fix for both prior rejections at once. VISIBLE LAYER (note density, lanes in play, cut axes, bomb rate): earned via THE CHAIN, displayed, player-selectable once unlocked, and NEVER auto-adjusted — mid-run or between runs. Silently scaling the thing the player measures themselves by is the Oblivion level-scaling failure, and it is precisely what produces 'no goal, stale after seconds'. INVISIBLE LAYER (never named, never shown, never scored): contact window, cut-angle tolerance, minimum swipe velocity, approach-time margin. Adapt this one on the Hades God Mode pattern — it ramps only on FAILURE, never on success, so improving can never be punished. Concrete numbers: cut-angle tolerance for a 4-axis kerf system starts at plus/minus 55 degrees off the required axis (deliberately overlapping quadrants — genuinely generous) and tightens to plus/minus 30 degrees by block 10; contact window around apex starts at plus/minus 150ms and tightens to plus/minus 90ms; the PERFECT band stays fixed at plus/minus 60ms forever so the ceiling never moves and skill expression stays honest. Adaptive step: after any run graded below C, widen the contact window by +8ms and angle tolerance by +3 degrees, capped at +40ms / +15 degrees total; halve the accumulated bonus on any B-or-better. Never announce it. This is the coyote-time contract — the calibration target is a player saying 'that miss was my fault', and note that the eye runs ~13ms behind reality, so a little invisible generosity is corrective, not charitable.
- 4. SHIP A TEN-RUNG LADDER AT x1.18 PER BLOCK, ANCHORED TO BEAT SABER'S PUBLISHED BANDS AND CHOP'S OWN 136 BPM. Beat Saber's official downmapping constant is ~20% density per tier; x1.18 gives a finer 10-rung climb over the same span. Per block: NPS / notes per 70s level / approach time / new vocabulary. B1 1.10 NPS / 77 notes / 1.40s / bottom row + horizontal only. B2 1.30 / 91 / 1.35s / + vertical. B3 1.53 / 107 / 1.30s / + top row (all 6 cells). B4 1.81 / 127 / 1.22s / + red tx (bombs). B5 2.13 / 149 / 1.15s / + diagonals. B6 2.52 / 176 / 1.05s / + doubles on one beat. B7 2.97 / 208 / 0.98s / + the long chop (hold-drag). B8 3.50 / 245 / 0.90s. B9 4.13 / 289 / 0.82s. B10 4.88 / 342 / 0.75s. B1's 1.10 NPS sits at the floor of Beat Saber's Easy band (0.82-2.29) and is exactly one note per two beats — the beginner-charting norm of quarter/half-note mapping to the strong beat. B10's 4.88 NPS matches Rhythm Quest's measured 4.8 presses/sec cap for a one-verb game and sits mid-Hard by Beat Saber's table (1.73-5.22), which is correct because a swipe costs 150-200ms of travel that a VR wrist flick does not. HARD FLOOR: never take approach time below 0.70s on a phone — Beat Saber's Expert+ 0.55s assumes two hands and no finger occluding the target. Above B10, do not keep adding density: open MEMPOOL, an endless mode that ramps from 3.5 NPS by +0.15 every 8 bars until the player breaks.
- 5. RAMP INSIDE EACH 70-SECOND BLOCK, NOT JUST ACROSS BLOCKS — AND PUT THE BLOCK'S REAL WHALES AT THE PEAK. Beatstar's defining structural choice is that songs carry no single difficulty: the chart level rises as you go deeper. Left 4 Dead proves a peak only reads as a peak if a trough precedes it. Chart every block as a five-phase curve multiplying the tier's base NPS: BARS 1-8 at 0.6x (the on-ramp — for B1 this doubles as the tutorial), BARS 9-20 at 1.0x, BARS 21-28 at 0.7x (the rest — this is where the player breathes and where the music strips back), BARS 29-38 at 1.35x (THE CLIMAX), BARS 39-40 at 0.4x (the outro / the seal ceremony). Weighted average lands at ~1.0x tier NPS. Then make the climax mean something bitcoin-native: sort the real block's transactions by fee rate and place the actual top-fee whales in bars 29-38. The level's hardest moment IS the block's biggest transactions. That is the block connection expressed as mechanics rather than as a caption, which is exactly what v2 was missing.
- 6. GIVE SKILL THREE LEGIBLE LAYERS — ONE A BEGINNER CAN HEAR, ONE THEY CAN SEE, ONE ONLY AN EXPERT CAN PLAY. Layer A, AUDIBLE (highest impact, cheapest): gate instrument layers on the combo multiplier in music.js, which currently only has setIntensity(1/2/3). Extend to five: x1 drums+bass, x2 +arp, x3 +lead, x5 +pad/choir, x8 the full mix plus a sub-drop on the eighth chop. Fruit Ninja's Blitz tiers do exactly this — the drums get louder per tier and the top tier modulates the drum roll — and it means someone watching over a shoulder can HEAR skill without reading a number. Layer B, VISUAL: make cut QUALITY visible in the artifact, not just in the HUD. A perfect-axis, perfect-timing cut produces a mirror-bright cut face and two halves that separate SYMMETRICALLY; a sloppy cut produces a dim ragged face and halves that tumble asymmetrically. Symmetry is legible to anyone. Layer C, THE EXPERT LAYER: bank a Star-Power analog. Chopping whales (feeNorm > 0.72) fills a HASHPOWER meter; the player chooses when to detonate it for a 6-second 2x window. Guitar Hero's SP PATHING generated an entire wiki subculture (ScoreHero) precisely because deploy-timing is deep, invisible to the chart, and lets an expert score 3x a beginner on the IDENTICAL note chart. That last property is what makes a shared daily leaderboard meaningful — and combo-with-a-full-clear beating raw accuracy is already the genre norm (90% accuracy with an FC outscores 99% with a break).
- 7. BUILD THE 'I AM GETTING BETTER' LOOP EXPLICITLY — IT DOES NOT EMERGE ON ITS OWN. Six mechanisms, in order of value. (a) SELF-GHOST: because CHOP's chart is deterministic per block, record the hit timeline (not frames) of your best run on that block and replay it as a second, dimmer blade trail. Improvement only becomes a feedback loop when the comparison is concrete. (b) PER-SKILL DELTAS on the clear card, never just a score: track 6 axes (horizontal, vertical, diagonal, doubles, whales, red-tx avoidance) and always surface the single best-improved one in plain language — 'vertical cuts +11%', 'longest chain 34, was 21', 'first clean whale cluster'. A raw score delta is arithmetic; a named skill delta is identity. (c) LIVE PB CALLOUT at bar 20 and bar 30: a quiet 'AHEAD OF BEST' tick when you are outpacing your own best on this block — near-miss tension is the whole engine of one-more-run. (d) THE CHAIN AS A CHART: under the sealed-block collection, draw a 20-run grade sparkline. Improvement you can see without doing maths, and it is the direct counter to the osu! 3-star plateau problem where players quit because progress became invisible. (e) RETIRE THE BASICS: once a player holds A-grade at tier N, stop ghost-demonstrating tier-N mechanics — the kart's coach pattern. Nothing communicates 'you got better' like the game visibly ceasing to treat you as new. (f) ZERO RESTART FRICTION: clear card to next run in ONE tap, under 400ms, music never stops. Super Hexagon's whole design thesis is that restarting must be fewer taps than quitting.
- 8. GATE TIERS AS EARNED-THEN-FREE, RUN LEADERBOARDS PER TIER, AND NEVER LOCK CONTENT. Default path (the normie): tiers auto-advance with THE CHAIN — seal block N, tier N+1 unlocks and is automatically selected next run. No menu, no self-assessment, no shame. Override path (the expert): from block 3 onward, every unlocked tier plus one is freely selectable, and once unlocked a tier is unlocked forever across all blocks — never re-earned. Expert players must not have to grind up a ladder they can already climb; newcomers must not be asked to rate themselves. NEVER lock content behind a tier: every block is fully playable and fully sealable at every tier — Cuphead's Simple mode cutting boss phases and withholding Soul Contracts is the canonical failure, and the sharpest critique of it applies verbatim here (nothing removed at low difficulty couldn't have been scaled back instead). STEP-DOWN IS OFFERED, NEVER IMPOSED: after two consecutive sub-C runs at the current tier, the clear card offers 'run this block one gear lower?' as a button. Automatic demotion is the Oblivion mistake — it deletes the feeling of having climbed. LEADERBOARDS ARE PER (BLOCK, TIER): 'BLOCK 927,418 - TIER 7'. A single all-comers board on a bitcoin-X launch would put every normie 400 places behind someone who has played 200 runs; per-tier boards mean a beginner's board is full of beginners, which is how Beatstar and osu! both handle it.
- 9. PUT THE OPTIONS UNDER 'DISPLAY', NOT 'DIFFICULTY', AND NAME THE ONE REAL ASSIST 'PRACTICE'. Four items, none of which reads as pity. (a) APPROACH SPEED: SLOW / NORMAL / FAST, decoupled entirely from note density — the rhythm community's settled position is that scroll speed is a readability preference, not a difficulty setting, and exposing FAST as well as SLOW is what makes it read as customisation. (b) KERF WEIGHT + HIGH CONTRAST: thicker, brighter cut-axis grooves — pure readability. (c) REDUCE FLASH: already implied by the build's reduceMotion path; surface it. (d) LEFT-HAND MIRROR. Then exactly ONE honestly-named assist: PRACTICE — half speed, any tier, unlimited, no board post, and the clear card reads 'PRACTICE - not sealed' rather than a grade. Naming it PRACTICE rather than EASY or CASUAL follows the de-stigmatisation pattern the whole industry converged on (Storyteller / Narrative / Story Time) and matches what every rhythm game already calls this. Copy discipline, learned from Celeste's public walk-back of the word 'essential': never write anything that implies a player using an option is not really playing CHOP. If a modifier must cost score, copy Beat Saber's revision — cost nothing until the assist is actually consumed.
- 10. INSTRUMENT THESE EXACT TARGETS AND TREAT THE SECOND-RUN RATE AS THE PRIMARY KPI. Run 1: block-seal rate 100% (structural, not tuned); median note hit-rate 88-92%; grade distribution ~68% B-or-better, ~10% A, under 2% S; time-to-first-slash under 4.0s; zero fails. Runs 2-5: median hit-rate 85-88% — this is where the Wilson 85% rule (optimal training error 15.87%) should bind, and where you should NOT chase a higher number, because a player succeeding at 95% is learning more slowly than one succeeding at 85%. Run 20 / block 10 at 4.88 NPS: hit-rate 80-85%, S-rank achieved by 5-8% of attempts. SECOND-RUN START RATE WITHIN THE SAME SESSION: target 55%+ — this is the metric that answers the operator's brief, it is measurable on day one, and it is far more diagnostic than any retention number. Session length: 4-5 blocks (~5 min), matching the top-25% mobile session of 5.2 min. Return-within-48h: 20%+ (mobile D1 median is ~20%, top-25% ~30%, top-10% ~40%, and arcade genres over-index on D1 while collapsing on D30 — THE CHAIN exists to attack that collapse). Long-run difficulty equilibrium: ~3.2 attempts per block clear once a player is 10+ blocks deep, matching the published mature-difficulty plateau — but ~1.0 attempts on day one. RED FLAG: if first-session completion falls under 30%, the opening needs rebuilding rather than tuning; 70% is the healthy line, and CHOP's no-fail construction should put it near 100%.
- 11. MAKE THE BLOCK CONNECTION MECHANICAL, USING DIFFICULTY ITSELF AS THE EXPLANATION. v2's rejection included 'no explanation of the block connection', and prose will not fix that. Use the tier as BLADE RESOLUTION: a real block carries roughly 2,000-4,000 transactions, so at B1's 77 chops each chop clears ~36 transactions, and at B10's 342 chops each clears ~8. Surface that in the HUD from second one — 'BLOCK 927,418 - 3,104 TX - 12 MIN AGO' — pop it per chop ('+2,140 sats - 38 tx'), and stamp it on the sealed artifact: 'SEALED AT 8 TX PER CHOP'. That single line makes the difficulty ladder legible AND bitcoin-native AND brag-worthy in one move: climbing the ladder literally means resolving the chain more finely. Combine with recommendation 5 (real top-fee whales charted into bars 29-38) and the boot card needs only one sentence of explanation — 'every block mined becomes a level; this one is 12 minutes old' — because the rest is demonstrated, not told, which is exactly how Rhythm Heaven and Pokemon Go teach.
- 12. THINGS TO EXPLICITLY NOT DO, EACH WITH ITS CAUTIONARY SOURCE. Do not auto-scale note density or lane count mid-run or between runs (Oblivion level scaling — it erases the feeling of having grown, and it is the mechanical root of 'no goal'). Do not rubber-band the score or silently inflate sats for a struggling player (Mario Kart — visible advantage-adjustment reads as rigged, and the score is the exact quantity players compare with friends on X). Do not gate any block, ending, or CHAIN entry behind a tier (Cuphead's Simple mode). Do not show a difficulty menu before run 1 (menu selection is an uninformed choice for someone who has never played). Do not open with a tutorial screen, modal, or calibration prompt — Rhythm Quest's own alpha found latency calibration 'very opaque' to first-timers who cannot verify a 30ms offset by eye; auto-calibrate silently from the first 8 bars of hits and only offer manual calibration in settings. Do not let a first-timer fail twice in five minutes (the sub-7% retry cliff). Do not label anything Easy, Casual, or Beginner. And do not make the invisible leniency layer visible in the score for a player who never needed it — Beat Saber's fix was to penalise only once the assist is actually consumed.

# WEB

Mobile web is a ~85–110 ms input-to-photon platform before you write a line of code — native mobile games measure 86–88 ms end-to-end on flagships ([GameBench](https://blog.gamebench.net/touch-latency-benchmarks-iphone-xs-max-galaxy-note-10)), and the browser adds at most one more frame. That single fact kills the tap-timing rhythm-game genre on phones (Arcaea-class windows are 25 ms) and simultaneously validates the operator's instinct: Beat Saber's 115-point score contains ZERO timing points — it is 70 pre-swing angle + 30 post-swing angle + 15 cut centrality, so a correctly-aimed swing scores 115 regardless of *when* it lands ([AccSaber time-dependence](https://wiki.accsaber.com/Beat_Saber/TD/)). If CHOP grades cut GEOMETRY (axis match, centrality, follow-through) and uses timing only as a coarse gate, mobile web latency becomes a non-issue. If CHOP grades timing accuracy, no amount of engineering will save it — Bluetooth alone injects 80–274 ms (AirPods Pro 2: 80–160 ms AAC; AirPods 1: 274 ms), iOS Low Power Mode silently halves rAF to 30 fps and is undetectable, and >91% of surveyed mobile players play muted anyway.

60 fps is achievable — this repo already ships a custom-WebGL2 3D racer on phones — but the achievable envelope is: 60 Hz (iOS Safari caps rAF at 60 by default), 10–12 ms frame budget at thermal equilibrium (not 16.6), <100 draw calls, <150 MB GPU memory, and a total page footprint under ~200–300 MB before Safari jetsams the tab. The current /public/chop build has nine concrete, fixable defects against these constraints — the most serious being no `getCoalescedEvents` (cut ANGLE is quantized to the frame rate on Chrome Android, which directly breaks the kerf/directional-cut mechanic), no iOS `"interrupted"` AudioContext handling (game goes permanently silent after any incoming call), no `navigator.audioSession` (silent for every ring-switch-muted iPhone), no `webglcontextlost` handler (black canvas after backgrounding on iOS 17+), and 751 KB of uncompressed Three.js vendor code against a 3-second abandonment cliff where 53% of mobile users bail.

## Findings

- INPUT LATENCY FLOOR — mobile is slow before the browser is involved. GameBench measured native mobile games with a synchronized high-speed camera rig: iPhone XS Max averaged 88 ms (5 frames) and Galaxy Note 10 86 ms (5 frames) across PUBG Mobile / Arena of Valor / QQ Speed, all matched at 60 fps median. Their own perception bands: 0–83 ms 'competition grade', 83–133 ms 'solid for mainstream', 133–167 ms 'non-reflex games only'. https://blog.gamebench.net/touch-latency-benchmarks-iphone-xs-max-galaxy-note-10 — Phoboslab's browser measurement (120 fps camera, canvas frame counter) found Mobile Safari added ~0 frames over an 83 ms baseline while desktop Chrome added 3 frames (~50 ms). https://phoboslab.org/log/2012/06/measuring-input-lag-in-browsers CONCLUSION: budget 85–110 ms input-to-photon on mobile web and stop trying to beat it. Native does not beat it either.
- POINTER EVENTS ARE FRAME-ALIGNED ON CHROME, NOT ON SAFARI — and this specifically breaks directional cuts. Chrome 60+ deliberately delays continuous events (pointermove/touchmove/mousemove/wheel) to fire immediately before the rAF callback (they measured 35% fewer hit tests). https://developer.chrome.com/blog/aligning-input-events — Nolan Lawson's cross-browser test matrix confirms touchmove aligns to rAF in Chrome and Firefox on Android but NOT in Safari on iOS. https://nolanlawson.com/2019/08/14/browsers-input-events-and-frame-throttling/ CONSEQUENCE FOR CHOP: on Chrome Android you receive roughly ONE pointermove per frame, so a 150 ms swipe yields ~9 samples and a fast flick yields 2–3 — the slash is a straight chord and the measured cut ANGLE is quantized by frame rate and swipe speed. A game whose core verb is 'match the kerf axis' cannot sample its input at 60 Hz. `getCoalescedEvents()` returns the true un-coalesced path and is NOW available on Safari 18.2+ / iOS Safari 18.2+ (previously unsupported), Chrome, and Samsung Internet 7.2+ — 92.8% global. https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_pointerevent_getcoalescedevents The current build (public/chop/js/main.js:1250-1253) listens to plain pointermove and never calls it.
- THE 300 ms TAP DELAY IS ALREADY SOLVED and the current build is correct here. Chrome 32+ and iOS 9.3+ drop the double-tap-zoom wait when the page declares `width=device-width`, and `touch-action: manipulation` opts in per-element. https://developer.chrome.com/blog/300ms-tap-delay-gone-away — public/chop/index.html:23 sets `width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=no, viewport-fit=cover` and style.css:4 sets `touch-action: none; overscroll-behavior: none`. No FastClick-class problem exists. Do not let anyone 'fix' this.
- WEB AUDIO LATENCY: iOS IS FINE, ANDROID IS A LOTTERY, BLUETOOTH IS A CATASTROPHE. Apple devices reach sub-10 ms output latency reliably; Android historically ranged 12.5 ms (Galaxy Nexus) to 150 ms on cheap hardware, and Android's own tiers define `low_latency` as ≤45 ms output and 'pro' as ≤20 ms round trip. Chrome's AAudio backend uses AAUDIO_PERFORMANCE_MODE_LOW_LATENCY for the 'interactive' latency profile; without it callbacks fall to ~40 ms intervals. https://developer.android.com/ndk/guides/audio/audio-latency — Bluetooth dwarfs all of it: measured AirPods 1 = 274 ms, AirPods 2 = 178 ms, AirPods Pro = 144 ms, AirPods Pro 2 = 80–160 ms over AAC; SBC/AAC generally >100 ms, aptX LL 30–40 ms. https://stephencoyle.net/airpods-pro and https://onlineaudiotest.com/devices/airpods-pro-2/ — Real `outputLatency` readings confirm the split: ~0.015 s (Firefox) / 0.024 s (Chrome) wired vs 0.178 s on Bluetooth. https://www.jamieonkeys.dev/posts/web-audio-api-output-latency/
- THE LATENCY APIs ARE NOW QUERYABLE ON iOS — the current code's assumption is stale. `AudioContext.baseLatency` ships Safari 14.1 / iOS Safari 14.5+ (94.53% global) https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_audiocontext_baselatency and `AudioContext.outputLatency` — the one that actually reflects Bluetooth — ships Safari 18.4 / iOS Safari 18.4+ plus Chrome and Samsung Internet 19+ (91.5% global). https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_audiocontext_outputlatency — public/chop/js/music.js:84 still carries the comment '(Safari/iOS lack outputLatency)' and falls back to `baseLatency`. On an iOS 18.3-or-older iPhone with AirPods, that fallback returns ~0.003 s as the correction for a listener who is genuinely ~150 ms late: wrong by two orders of magnitude, and the game will feel like it is scoring the player's swings a fifth of a second after they happen.
- iOS SILENTLY MUTES WEB AUDIO WHEN THE RINGER SWITCH IS OFF. This is WebKit bug 237322 and it is not a bug Apple is fixing — Web Audio defaults to the 'ambient' session category, which the hardware mute switch silences. https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=237322 — Two fixes exist: the standards path `navigator.audioSession.type = 'playback'` (W3C Audio Session, implemented in Safari ONLY, which is fine because it is only Safari that has the problem) https://www.w3.org/TR/audio-session/ and the legacy silent-HTMLAudio-loop trick that forces WebAudio onto the media channel (feross/unmute-ios-audio, swevans/unmute). The current build does neither. For a rhythm game whose entire pitch is 'slash to the beat', shipping silent to the large fraction of iPhone users who keep the ringer muted is an unrecoverable first impression.
- AN INCOMING CALL PERMANENTLY KILLS THE CURRENT BUILD'S AUDIO. iOS puts AudioContext into a NON-STANDARD `'interrupted'` state on incoming calls, browser dialogs, headphone removal, and backgrounding — a state that does not exist in the Web Audio spec. Code that only checks `state === 'suspended'` never recovers. https://github.com/WebAudio/web-audio-api/issues/2585 and https://github.com/Tonejs/Tone.js/issues/767 — public/chop/js/music.js:450 does exactly this (`if (ctx.state === 'suspended') ctx.resume();`), and main.js:1487's visibilitychange handler calls Music.start() on return, which will not clear an interrupted context. Some reports have contexts stuck in `interrupted` even after `resume()` until a fresh user gesture arrives.
- MEMORY: SAFARI KILLS TABS EARLIER THAN ANYONE EXPECTS, AND THE LIMIT IS DEVICE-DEPENDENT. Empirical testing on iOS 26.2 crashed pages at ~100 MB on an iPhone SE (3rd gen) and ~200 MB on an iPad (8th gen), sometimes freezing or rebooting the device rather than just killing the tab — and the failure is UNCATCHABLE (no exception, the page just dies). https://lapcatsoftware.com/articles/2026/1/7.html — Older empirical figures: iPhone 6 ≈645 MB, 6s ≈1 GB, 7 ≈2 GB, with modern iPhones jetsammed at an ActiveHard limit of 2048 MB; the commonly-cited practical web-page budget is 300–450 MB. Three.js guidance for mobile puts GPU memory at <150 MB. The relevant number for CHOP: procedurally generated music (public/chop/js/music.js, 25 KB of code) sidesteps the decodeAudioData trap entirely — a decoded 10-minute 44.1 kHz stereo float buffer is ~212 MB, and a 70-second track is still ~27 MB of float32. Keeping the music synthesized is a genuine structural advantage; do not trade it for a .mp3.
- WEBGL CONTEXT LOSS IS ROUTINE ON MOBILE AND THE BUILD HAS NO HANDLER. iOS 17+ loses the WebGL context when Safari is backgrounded and refocused (WebKit bug 261331 — the canvas renders nothing until a full reload); Chrome Android reclaims GPU memory from backgrounded tabs and invalidates the context on restore; Chrome also caps ~16 simultaneous WebGL contexts per tab. https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=261331 — Correct handling is `webglcontextlost` with `preventDefault()`, then rebuild on `webglcontextrestored`: recompile shaders, re-upload textures, rebuild VBOs from JS-side cached data. public/chop/js/main.js has no such listener, so the realistic mobile flow 'start a level → get a notification → come back' ends in a black screen.
- FRAME RATE IS NOT YOURS TO CHOOSE. (a) iOS Safari renders at 60 fps by default and only exceeds it if the user manually disables the 'Prefer Page Rendering Updates near 60fps' feature flag (Safari 26.3) — so on iPhone, plan for exactly 60 Hz. https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/enable-smoother-120hz-browsing-in-safari/ (b) iOS Low Power Mode throttles rAF to 30 fps and is effectively UNDETECTABLE from JS (WebKit bugs 168837 and 215745). https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=168837 (c) Android 90/120 Hz panels run rAF at 90/120, cutting the frame budget to 11 ms / 8.3 ms. CONSEQUENCE: the game clock must be the audio clock and every sim step must be dt-scaled — which main.js:1500 and music.js's `heardTime()` already do correctly. Keep that; it is the single most important thing the current build got right.
- THERMAL THROTTLING IS THE REAL FRAME BUDGET. Throttling onset is 2–5 minutes into gameplay on flagships and 60–90 SECONDS on budget phones; sustained performance settles at 55–70% of peak (flagship), 50–65% (mid-range), 40–55% (budget). A measured Android case dropped 35 fps → 23 fps (34% degradation). Android's own guidance shows a device that renders 60 fps for a few minutes throttling to 30 fps by minute 30, versus a stable 45 fps in sustained-performance mode. https://source.android.com/docs/core/power/performance and https://www.abratabia.com/mobile-browser-performance/battery-and-thermal.php — Practical targets from the same source: design to 60–70% of peak GPU, budget 10–12 ms per frame (not 16.6), test 10–15 minutes continuously. A 70-second level is genuinely well-suited to this: it lands inside the pre-throttle window on flagships. A 20-level session does not.
- BATTERY: A WEBGL GAME AT FULL GPU DRAWS 4–7 W, which empties a 4500 mAh (≈17 Wh) battery in 2.5–4 hours of continuous play; 60 fps costs 1.5–2× the power of 30 fps for the same scene; OLED panels draw 1–2 W and bright content costs more than dark. Android's app-quality bar flags >4.44%/hour on battery as excessive. A dark, orange-on-black palette is materially cheaper on OLED than a bright one. https://www.abratabia.com/mobile-browser-performance/battery-and-thermal.php — WebKit's own power guidance: when a page is not visible, rAF stops, CSS/SVG animations suspend, timers throttle, and on iOS 'tabs are completely suspended when possible'. https://webkit.org/blog/8970/how-web-content-can-affect-power-usage/
- BACKGROUNDING / INCOMING CALLS: THE PAGE CAN BE FROZEN OR DISCARDED OUTRIGHT. Page Lifecycle takes hidden pages to FROZEN (timers, callbacks, DOM work stopped) and typically then to DISCARDED, at which point returning to the tab RELOADS the page; `document.wasDiscarded` is how you detect it. https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/page-lifecycle-api — For CHOP this means: a run in progress can vanish entirely. Any score, streak, or CHAIN progress must be written to localStorage synchronously at the moment it is earned, before any network call — the same 'never trust an unsent outbox' discipline already documented for the arcade cloud save.
- SCREEN SLEEP: Wake Lock works but has a version cliff. `navigator.wakeLock.request('screen')` is supported in Safari on iOS 16.4+ (partial 16.4–16.5, full 16.6+) and everywhere on Android, but it was BROKEN in installed iOS PWAs until iOS 18.4. https://caniuse.com/wake-lock and https://webkit.org/blog/13966/webkit-features-in-safari-16-4/ — A 70-second level with a low-touch-frequency section will hit the display timeout on default settings without it. The current build does not request it.
- NO HAPTICS ON iPHONE, PERIOD. `navigator.vibrate` is not implemented in Safari on any iOS version — Apple's stated reason is abuse potential. The only documented programmatic haptic is the iOS 18+ non-standard `<input type=checkbox switch>` hack: programmatically `click()` its associated `<label>` and Safari emits a haptic. https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Navigator/vibrate — public/chop/js/main.js:1098 `hapticSlice()` bails on `!navigator.vibrate`, so the entire haptic feedback ladder (whale 3-pulse, milestone double-tap) is dead on 100% of iPhones. Half the intended audience gets a strictly weaker impact feel than the other half, and it is not recoverable — the visual+audio impact language has to carry it alone.
- PWA/INSTALL IS THE WRONG BET FOR AN X-SOURCED AUDIENCE. iOS has no `beforeinstallprompt`; every install is a manual Add-to-Home-Screen from Safari, and that option is missing or degraded inside in-app browsers — which is exactly where a link tapped in the X app opens. WKWebView-based in-app browsers have their own WebGL history (a documented iOS 15 regression halved WebGL frame rate in WKWebView, 60 → 30 fps, tied to the 'GPU Process: Canvas Rendering' feature). https://www.mobiloud.com/blog/progressive-web-apps-ios and https://github.com/apache/cordova-ios/issues/1246 — Design the first session to be perfect inside a WebView with zero install, and treat A2HS as an upsell after a good run (the Kart's existing A2HS funnel pattern).
- LOAD TIME IS A HARDER GATE THAN FRAME RATE. 53% of mobile users abandon a page taking over 3 seconds; bounce probability rises 32% going 1s→3s and 90% going 1s→5s. https://www.marketingdive.com/news/google-53-of-mobile-users-abandon-sites-that-take-over-3-seconds-to-load/426070/ — The current CHOP build ships 816 KB of `vendor/` (three.core.min.js 385 KB + three.module.min.js 366 KB + the postprocessing/shader chain) plus 94 KB of game code = ~924 KB uncompressed. For comparison, Bitcoin Kart in this same repo is 852 KB TOTAL including PWA icons, with a hand-rolled WebGL2 renderer and ZERO runtime dependencies, against a documented 560 KB minified payload cap. Three.js is buying convenience at roughly the cost of the entire Kart.
- THE RENDER SETUP IS THE BIGGEST FILL-RATE RISK AND CONTAINS ONE WASTED FEATURE. public/chop/js/main.js:82-104 creates `WebGLRenderer({ antialias: true, powerPreference: 'high-performance' })`, sets pixel ratio to `min(devicePixelRatio, 2)`, then routes everything through `EffectComposer` + `UnrealBloomPass`. On a 393×852 CSS iPhone at DPR 2 that is 786×1704 ≈ 1.34 M pixels; UnrealBloom adds a luminosity pass plus 5 mip levels × 2 separable blur draws plus a composite ≈ 11–13 additional full/partial-screen passes. And `antialias: true` is largely wasted once the composer owns the render target. Mobile Three.js guidance: <100 draw calls (ideally <50), 50–100 K on-screen triangles, <150 MB GPU memory, and post-processing gated behind device capability because full-screen passes can double frame time. https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-best-practices-100-tips There is no quality tier, no fps probe, and no reduceFx path in the build — the repo's own Kart already has all three.
- GC IS A RHYTHM-GAME KILLER AND THE BUILD IS PARTIALLY DEFENDED. At 60 fps a single 50 ms GC pause drops three frames — on a note-grid game that is a guaranteed miss the player did not earn. The failure signature is a memory sawtooth, not a flame-chart spike, so it is invisible to naive profiling. Object pooling reduces GC pressure by ~90% in per-frame-allocation patterns. https://renderlog.in/blog/javascript-garbage-collection-frontend/ — main.js:1219 shows the right instinct ('scratch for pointer path (avoid per-move Vector3 allocs)'), but the discipline needs to cover notes, debris, particles, and the score popups, all of which are per-note churn.
- PLAYERS DO NOT COMPLETE CALIBRATION WIZARDS, AND THE ONES WHO DO OFTEN DON'T TRUST THEM. Documented complaints: players skip the offset test outright; in-game calibration 'doesn't work that well'; offsets that differ on each restart destroy confidence; and forcing zero offset regardless of system latency is 'off-putting, especially to casual players'. https://steamcommunity.com/app/774181/discussions/0/3200370144982666969/ — The structural limit nobody can engineer around: tapping to AUDIO measures audio+input latency, tapping to VISUALS measures video+input latency; you can only ever recover the A/V OFFSET from the difference, never isolate input lag. Rhythm Quest concluded the same and shipped a single screen with only FOUR taps plus a visual A/B adjuster, because 'the human eye is very keen at determining slight differences in timing between two flashing objects'. https://rhythmquestgame.com/devlog/10.html — Native Audio's crash course adds the pro conventions: preset a sane default so a non-calibrating player still has an acceptable experience, offer calibration AFTER the tutorial rather than as a gate, and bake a 'magic number' (a UI-zero that is really 5–10 ms) to account for air travel. https://exceed7.com/native-audio/rhythm-game-crash-course/index.html
- THE STATE OF THE ART IN CALIBRATION IS NOT A WIZARD — IT IS INFERENCE FROM REAL PLAY. osu!'s Offset Wizard has the player play a real map and then sets their offset to their MEAN HIT ERROR; Quaver ships the same 'Calibrate Offset' idea. https://osu.ppy.sh/community/forums/topics/841149 — This is the pattern that actually completes, because the completion rate of 'play the game' is 100%. For CHOP this maps cleanly: after one 70-second block, you have 60–120 cut samples; the median signed error IS the player's total system offset, and its spread tells you whether to trust it.
- MOST OF YOUR PLAYERS WILL BE MUTED, WHICH IS AN ARGUMENT ABOUT INFORMATION CHANNELS, NOT ABOUT AUDIO QUALITY. Survey data is genuinely contradictory — TapResearch's 2,400-person survey found >91% play with sound off and only ~8% with sound on, while other surveys report 73% play at ≥10% volume and ~60% 'play with sound regularly'; one figure holds across all of them: only ~3% of players worldwide use headphones. https://blog.tapresearch.com/how-sound-preferences-impact-player-engagement — Combine that with the iOS ringer-switch mute and the reality of a link tapped from X at a desk or in public, and a very large share of FIRST sessions are silent. Accessibility practice points the same way: represent rhythm visually in the interface itself (Cadence of Hyrule pulses enemies to the beat) so the audio amplifies information the player can already see. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/deaf-accessibility-in-video-games
- WHAT CHOP ALREADY GETS RIGHT — worth protecting from a future refactor: the game time is derived from the AudioContext clock with a latency correction (`heardTime = currentTime - latencyOffset()`, music.js:96-100) rather than from frame count or performance.now(); the music is code-generated so there is no decodeAudioData memory cliff and the exact note grid is known ahead of time; a 100 ms lookahead scheduler is in place (music.js:439), matching the canonical 'A Tale of Two Clocks' pattern of ~100 ms lookahead with 25 ms intervals https://web.dev/articles/audio-scheduling ; hitstop counts down on wall clock so the freeze can always expire while audio keeps running; and the viewport/touch-action/overscroll setup is textbook-correct.

## Recommendations

- SCORE GEOMETRY, NOT TIMING — this is the whole ballgame and it is free. Copy Beat Saber's mechanism, not just its 115: 70 pts for cut-axis match against the kerf (angular error, generous falloff), 30 pts for follow-through past the block, 15 pts for centrality of the cut line through the face. Timing then only decides WHETHER the note is live, via a fat corridor window (recommend ±120 ms early / ±100 ms late, i.e. ~7 frames each way — roughly the note's visual depth in the corridor), and contributes ZERO points. This makes an 85–110 ms platform latency, a 30 fps Low Power Mode session, and a 144 ms AirPods listener all playable without the player ever knowing. It also gives you the skill ceiling the operator wants (perfect axis + centrality + follow-through is genuinely hard) without ever needing a 25 ms window you cannot deliver. AccSaber's time-dependence math is the proof this works: a swing along the line of centers scores 115 regardless of when it lands.
- ADOPT getCoalescedEvents() FOR THE SLASH PATH — highest-impact single code change for the core verb. In the pointermove handler, iterate `e.getCoalescedEvents()` (feature-detected, fall back to the single event) and feed EVERY sample into the trail/cut-test. Without it, Chrome Android delivers ~1 sample per frame and your measured cut angle is quantized by frame rate and swipe speed — fatal for a game whose identity is directional cuts. Supported Safari/iOS 18.2+, Chrome, Samsung 7.2+ (92.8% global). Do the cut-vs-note intersection against the polyline of coalesced samples, not against the last two dispatched points. Optionally add a Chrome-only `pointerrawupdate` path for one extra notch of responsiveness.
- FIX THE THREE AUDIO LIFECYCLE BUGS BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE — each one is a total-failure mode for a subset of users. (a) Set `navigator.audioSession.type = 'playback'` when available, plus the silent-HTMLAudio-loop fallback, or every ring-switch-muted iPhone gets a silent rhythm game. (b) Treat `'interrupted'` as a first-class state everywhere you currently check `'suspended'` (music.js:450) and re-attempt `resume()` on the next real user gesture, not just on visibilitychange — otherwise one phone call ends audio for the session. (c) Update the stale `latencyOffset()` comment and prefer `outputLatency` (now on iOS 18.4+), because on Bluetooth it returns ~0.178 s where `baseLatency` returns ~0.003 s; also re-read it on `devicechange`/route change and re-offer calibration when it jumps by >50 ms.
- REPLACE THE CALIBRATION WIZARD WITH INFERENCE FROM THE FIRST BLOCK — the only calibration UX with a 100% completion rate. Seed the offset from `outputLatency` → `baseLatency` → a device-class default so the first run is already acceptable and NEVER gate play on calibration. During block #1 record the signed timing error of every cut relative to the note's ideal moment. At the results screen, if |median error| > 35 ms and the spread is tight, show one line and one button: 'You're cutting 62 ms early — snap the chart to your timing?' with a single Yes. Persist per audio-output route. This is exactly osu!'s Offset Wizard (offset := mean hit error) but harvested from a run the player wanted to do anyway. Keep a manual 4-tap + visual A/B fallback in settings for the minority who ask for it — Rhythm Quest's finding is that 4 taps and an eyeball A/B beats a 10-tap test.
- SHIP A QUALITY TIER + LIVE FPS PROBE, using the pattern this repo already proved in Bitcoin Kart (quality tiers, `reduceFx`, `fxScale`, `dofOk`-style capability fallbacks). Probe median frame time over the first 3–5 seconds of the opening block and pick a tier; then keep watching, because thermal throttling means the tier that was right at second 10 is wrong at minute 8 (mid-range phones sustain 50–65% of peak, budget phones 40–55%). Tier levers in priority order: DPR (clamp 1.25–1.5, not 2), bloom resolution (quarter-res, or drop the composer entirely on low tier), particle/debris counts, shadow-ish effects. Target 10–12 ms per frame at thermal equilibrium — not 16.6 — and honour `prefers-reduced-motion` (main.js:63 already reads it).
- CUT THE RENDER SETUP'S DEAD WEIGHT. Drop `antialias: true` — it does essentially nothing once EffectComposer owns the render target (set MSAA `samples` on the composer's target instead, on WebGL2 tiers where you can afford it). Clamp DPR to 1.5 by default with 2 reserved for the top tier. Move bloom to a quarter-resolution pass or hand-roll a 2-pass bloom to replace UnrealBloomPass's ~11–13 screen passes. Enforce budgets explicitly: <100 draw calls (aim <50), 50–100 K on-screen triangles, <150 MB GPU memory. A dark palette is not just brand — it is measurably cheaper on OLED.
- ADD THE THREE MISSING LIFECYCLE HANDLERS. (a) `webglcontextlost` with `preventDefault()` + `webglcontextrestored` that rebuilds shaders/textures/buffers from JS-cached data — iOS 17+ loses the context on backgrounding and Chrome Android reclaims it under memory pressure, so today 'notification mid-level' equals 'black screen'. (b) A real PAUSE state on `visibilitychange`/`freeze` that stops the run, shows a resume button, and re-arms audio inside that button's gesture handler — never silently resume a rhythm level the player was not watching. (c) `navigator.wakeLock.request('screen')` on level start with re-acquire on visibility return, so a 70-second level does not fight the display timeout.
- SLASH THE PAYLOAD TOWARD THE KART'S BENCHMARK. 924 KB uncompressed today (816 KB of it Three.js vendor, including what looks like BOTH `three.core.min.js` and `three.module.min.js`) against a cliff where 53% of mobile users abandon at 3 seconds. Concrete steps: ship one Three build not two; bundle+tree-shake rather than serving raw vendor modules; drop the EffectComposer/Pass/Shader chain if bloom becomes a hand-rolled pass; target <250 KB brotli to first interactive. If after that the budget still does not close, the fallback is the Kart's own answer — a hand-rolled WebGL2 renderer with zero runtime deps, which fits an entire 3D racer in 852 KB.
- MAKE THE GAME FULLY PLAYABLE MUTED, AND TREAT AUDIO AS AMPLIFICATION. Given >91% muted in one survey, ~3% headphone use globally, and the iOS ringer-switch mute, the approach corridor itself must be the metronome: beat-synced world pulses, a visible downbeat, kerf grooves readable at a glance, and note spacing that reads as rhythm with the sound at zero. Then let audio add impact rather than carry information. This is also the accessibility-correct design (visual representation of rhythm in the interface, per deaf-accessibility guidance) and it removes an entire class of first-session failure.
- POOL EVERYTHING PER-NOTE. Notes, debris, sparks, score popups, and the trail geometry should come from pre-allocated pools — one 50 ms GC pause drops three frames and, in a note-grid game, manufactures a miss the player did not earn. Extend the discipline already visible at main.js:1219 (scratch vectors for the pointer path) across the whole per-note lifecycle, and profile for the memory sawtooth specifically, not just for flame-chart spikes.
- DESIGN THE SESSION SHAPE AROUND THERMALS. A ~70-second block is a genuinely good unit: it fits inside the pre-throttle window on flagships (onset 2–5 min) though not on budget phones (60–90 s). Use the gaps between blocks — the CHAIN screen, the seal animation, the share card — as real cooldown: drop to a low-cost render mode or stop rAF entirely between levels rather than idling a full 3D scene. Verify with a 10–15 minute continuous-play test on a real mid-range Android, since anything shorter will not show the throttle.
- PERSIST PROGRESS SYNCHRONOUSLY AT THE MOMENT IT IS EARNED. A frozen page can be DISCARDED and reload on return (`document.wasDiscarded`), and iOS suspends tabs aggressively. Write the sealed block to localStorage before any leaderboard POST, and on boot reconcile — the same durable-only, never-replay-a-stale-outbox contract the arcade cloud save already documents. THE CHAIN is the meta-progression; losing one link to a phone call would be the worst possible bug in the whole design.
- BUILD FOR THE IN-APP BROWSER FIRST, NOT THE INSTALLED PWA. A bitcoin-X audience arrives via a link tapped inside the X app, which means WKWebView on iOS (no Add-to-Home-Screen option, its own WebGL history including a documented 60→30 fps WKWebView regression) and Android WebView/Chrome Custom Tabs. The first session must be flawless with zero install, zero permission prompts, and one tap to start (which is also the gesture that unlocks the AudioContext). Offer A2HS only AFTER a satisfying run, the way the Kart already does.
- PUT NUMERIC INVARIANTS IN THE TEST SUITE, since this repo enforces design decisions as tests. Candidates: hit windows never tighter than the agreed ms floor; the score function contains no timing term; every note's cut-axis tolerance sits inside the achievable band; the pointer handler calls getCoalescedEvents when available; latencyOffset prefers outputLatency; a payload-size ceiling for public/chop (the Kart's minified-cap pattern); and an audio-lifecycle assertion that every 'suspended' check also handles 'interrupted'. That converts this whole research memo into something that cannot silently regress.
- MEASURE IT ON REAL HARDWARE, AND KNOW THE TOOLING LIES. Two caveats already recorded in this project's memory apply directly: the browser pane never fires IntersectionObserver or rAF callbacks (document.hidden = true), so canvas/WebGL games render black there — verify via chrome-devtools MCP against real Chrome; and for latency specifically, the honest measurement is a 240 fps phone-camera capture of finger-contact-to-pixel (each frame ≈ 4.17 ms), the same method GameBench used. Anything derived from in-page timestamps measures your code, not the platform.

# TEARDOWN

Competitive teardown of 10+ rhythm/slicing titles judged as products, against the goal "the most addictive and satisfying bitcoin game ever made."

The decisive finding is that CHOP has been benchmarking itself against the wrong axis. Every timing-scored rhythm game it admires runs judgment windows of 15–59ms (DDR 15ms, Arcaea 25ms, Deemo 50ms, Dynamix 59ms). Mobile web cannot deliver that: `AudioContext.outputLatency` is unimplemented in WebKit, so on iOS — every browser on iOS is WebKit — CHOP's current fallback chain in `<repo>/public/chop/js/music.js` (`outputLatency` → `baseLatency` → `0`) returns **0 compensation** against a real output path that is routinely 100ms+, and 178ms on Bluetooth. A timing-accuracy game is not buildable here and every attempt will feel "unfair."

But Beat Saber — the game CHOP already borrowed its 115-point number from — **does not score timing at all**. Its 115 is 70 (100° approach angle) + 30 (60° follow-through) + 15 (cut through center); a player scores identically hitting a block early or late in the window. That is a pure-geometry scoring model, and geometry is the one thing a browser measures perfectly. CHOP's "kerf" directional cut is therefore not a stylistic flourish — it is the only defensible scoring axis on this platform, and it converts CHOP's worst technical liability into its design identity.

The second finding: v2's rejection notes ("no goal, stale after seconds") describe a meta-progression failure, not a moment-to-moment one. Every retention engine studied is a collection (Beatstar song cards, Cytus II character levels, Arcaea Potential, Phigros RKS/chapters) and every one of them is gated by a mechanic players openly resent — Beatstar's 3-hour case timers with 3 slots, Arcaea's 12-cap stamina and ~70% paywalled songs. CHOP's THE CHAIN is structurally stronger than all of them because its scarcity is real and free: a block arrives roughly every 10 minutes, can be sealed once, and is yours permanently. That is Wordle's appointment-scarcity running on a clock nobody controls — an engine no competitor can copy.

Third: Beatstar earned $73M in year one, hit 1M DAU and 38M downloads, and still shut down on 2025-10-31. Its entire architecture — no practice mode, hard case-slot gating, no unlimited play — existed to control per-play music licensing cost. CHOP's code-generated soundtrack means infinite free replay, which is the single largest structural advantage available in this category, and it should never be traded away.

## Findings

- BEAT SABER — the most important teardown, because CHOP already inherited its scoring number without inheriting its scoring philosophy. HOOK (10s): an instantly legible corridor where the required motion is pre-drawn on every target, so a first-timer's very first swing is correct and feels expert. RETENTION: it is a motion game wearing a rhythm game's clothes. Per bsmg.wiki, max score per block is 115 = 70 for a 100° approach swing + 30 for a 60° follow-through + 15 for cutting through the block's center. Timing contributes ZERO points — Road to VR's teardown confirms players score identically whether they hit a block immediately or at the last moment in the window. Multiplier ladder 1x→2x→4x→8x; ranks SS 90%+, S 80%, A 65%, B 50%, C 35%, D 20%, E <20%. COMPLAINT: none material at the mechanic level. THE ONE THING IT DOES BEST: 'instructed motion' — the arrows exist so the player does not flail; without them, movement is chaotic and the flow state never forms. The chart choreographs the body, and the fun is the choreography, not the timing. IMPLICATION FOR CHOP: this is the only rhythm-scoring model that survives mobile-web latency, and CHOP's kerf grooves are already 80% of the way there.
- TIMING WINDOWS ARE A HARD PLATFORM WALL — the single most consequential technical finding. Per exceed7.com's rhythm-game crash course, shipped judgment windows are DDR 15ms (one frame at 60fps), Arcaea 25ms, Deemo 50ms, Dynamix 59ms — and these are deliberately aligned to frame boundaries. Meanwhile jamieonkeys.dev documents that `AudioContext.outputLatency` is implemented in Firefox and Chrome desktop but NOT in WebKit (there is a literal fixme in the WebKit source), and because every iOS browser is WebKit, no iPhone can report it. Observed real values: 0.015–0.025s wired, 0.178s on Bluetooth headphones. I verified CHOP's current chain at public/chop/js/music.js:84-97 — `outputLatency` → `baseLatency` → `0`. On iOS that resolves to 0, so `heardTime()` equals raw context time and every iPhone player is being judged against uncompensated audio. Separately, touch events do not carry reliable hardware timestamps, so judgment resolves at frame time, which systematically advantages early-hitters. CONCLUSION: any CHOP scoring axis that resolves on milliseconds will be perceived as broken, and the perception will be correct.
- BEATSTAR — the cautionary tale, and the best-documented retention engine in the category. HOOK (10s): three lanes, a real licensed hit song, and a first tap that lands; note grades are named 'Perfect+/Perfect/Great' where 'Great' is actually the miss — the game refuses to say the word. RETENTION: song cards from timed cases (3hr → 2-4 cards; 5hr → 6-8), only 3 case slots, 3 cards to unlock a song early-game rising to 30 late; Tour Pass season pass; Daily Shuffle; 20-24hr tournament cycles in pools of 10-20 players; friends leaderboard shown before every song plus in-game 'brags'. NUMBERS: $73M revenue and $16M to rightsholders in year one, 38M downloads, 1M DAU, 2.5B song plays; RPD climbed $0.10 → $0.90 (≈$1.50 with ads); the Tour Pass lifted IAP ARPDAU 37% MoM from $0.11 to $0.14, then declined, and the price was cut $15 → $10 to arrest it. COMPLAINTS: 'horrible predatory monetization'; the 3-slot case gate literally stops play; Naavik's own verdict is 'a severe lack of systematic variation plagues its live-ops calendar' and 'there is no way for them to come up with fun and emergent strategies anywhere.' THE ONE THING IT DOES BEST: converting a song into a collectible — the song is not content, it is a card you assemble. FATE: shut down 2025-10-31 after Duolingo acqui-hired NextBeat. The architectural tell is that no practice mode and no unlimited play existed because per-play licensing made free repetition a cost center.
- MUSE DASH — the aesthetic-velocity play. HOOK (10s): a side-scrolling runner where you are already attacking things; two buttons (ground hit, air hit) and the entire control surface is learned in one bar of music. There is no lane-reading tax at all. RETENTION: thin by design — character/elfin unlocks and difficulty ascension, not a systems economy. NUMBERS: 2–5M Steam owners, all-time peak 11,402 concurrent, Steambase player score 91/100 across 120,959 reviews, base game ~$3 with 40+ songs. COMPLAINTS: the DLC economics — 'Muse Plus' and custom-chart access run ~$30 against a $3 base, and Steam threads call the base 'pretty much a demo'; a Hololive/publisher political incident triggered review-bombing. THE ONE THING IT DOES BEST: collapsing the input vocabulary to two verbs while keeping the fantasy loud. The visual noise is enormous but the decision space is binary, which is why it reads as effortless. DIRECT LESSON FOR CHOP: CHOP's 3 lanes × 2 rows × directional kerf is a 48-state decision space at a glance. Muse Dash proves the ceiling can stay high with a 2-state decision space if the presentation carries the intensity.
- CYTUS II — the best meta-progression in the genre, and the closest analog to what CHOP's THE CHAIN wants to be. HOOK (10s): a moving scanline instead of falling notes — you read a clock, not traffic. Novel enough to hold a first session on curiosity alone. RETENTION: the iM system, an in-fiction social-media feed. Characters (DJs) have levels; XP accrues from ANY play, including plays that set no new record and no new combo — so grinding a song you love is never wasted. Levelling drops new iM posts with likes and comment threads that carry the story, and certain posts stay locked until named characters hit a level, at which point that character stops gaining XP. COMPLAINTS: DLC at $9.99 per pack against a $1.99 base is widely called disproportionate; story pacing is gated behind grind. THE ONE THING IT DOES BEST: making progression legible as narrative rather than as a bar. The player is not filling a meter, they are unlocking a conversation. LESSON FOR CHOP: reward every play, not every good play — that single rule is why Cytus II retains, and it is the direct antidote to v2's 'stale after seconds.'
- ARCAEA — the skill-rating benchmark, and the clearest example of a retention engine that players tolerate rather than enjoy. HOOK (10s): a 3D two-plane corridor where the top lane's blue/pink 'arcs' assign each hand a color — a genuinely novel readability idea. RETENTION: Potential (PTT), an uncapped numerical skill rating that rises with new content and gates access to the hardest charts; World Mode maps consuming stamina; 40+ partners with stats and levels; Story Mode chapters unlocked by song requirements; 5 base difficulties (Past/Present/Future/Eternal/Beyond) rated 1-12 with '+' variants plus per-chart 'chart constants' for sub-level precision. COMPLAINTS (well-documented and consistent): stamina regenerates 1 unit per 30 minutes and stops accumulating at 12, so World Mode is described as forced slow play; roughly 70% of songs and the entire main and side story sit behind paid 'Memories', with the full story costing on the order of £30; the Switch version simply deleted stamina, which players read as an admission. THE ONE THING IT DOES BEST: the Potential rating — a single portable number that means something across the whole library, which turns every play into a stake on identity. That is the mechanic CHOP should study hardest for THE CHAIN.
- PHIGROS — the control case that proves free-and-ungated wins on sentiment. HOOK (10s): 'laneless' — the judgment line itself moves, rotates and multiplies, so the first 10 seconds are visually unlike anything else in the category. RETENTION: RKS skill rating plus chapter/story unlocks, and 200+ licensed tracks. MONETIZATION: none. No IAP, no ads, and the licensing on at least one track ('Aleph-0') contractually requires the game remain not-for-profit. NUMBERS: 4.5/5 across 33,850 Google Play reviews and 4.8/5 across 2,709 iOS ratings — the highest sentiment of any title in this teardown, achieved with zero monetization systems. COMPLAINT: the moving judgment line is polarizing; readability failures are experienced as unfairness rather than as difficulty. THE ONE THING IT DOES BEST: proving that removing every gate raises rating and reach simultaneously. CHOP is also free with no licensing cost, so Phigros — not Beatstar — is the correct sentiment benchmark to aim at.
- FRUIT NINJA (and the Fruit Ninja 2 anti-pattern) — CHOP's verb, and its clearest warning. HOOK (10s): universally legible in one gesture; no tutorial has ever been required. NUMBERS: 300M downloads by 2012 — installed on roughly one third of all US iPhones — and 1B by its fifth birthday in 2015; 1.8B across Halfbrick's catalog. The prototype took about six weeks. THE ONE THING IT DOES BEST: hidden generosity in the hitboxes. The bomb's real detonation volume is SMALLER than its visual, and the fruit's hit volume is LARGER than its visual, so near-misses convert into wins and near-hits on hazards convert into escapes. Combined with directional juice spray that persists and fades along the actual cut vector, this is why a 2010 game still out-feels most 2026 ones. FRUIT NINJA 2 ANTI-PATTERN: the sequel added soft currency, ad-doubled rewards, $10-15 unlock prompts, and shipped with only Arcade mode when the original offered every mode from the start — reviewers described everything as paywalled or time-gated. It is the same regression Beatstar made, and both were rejected by the same audience for the same reason: a slicing game's whole promise is uninterrupted flow, and every gate is an attack on the promise.
- FREE WEB TITLES — the distribution reality CHOP actually lives in. FRIDAY NIGHT FUNKIN': the first Newgrounds submission ever to pass 50M views, crashed Newgrounds on a major update, raised $2M+ on Kickstarter, and accumulated 4.4B TikTok views. Its engine compiled natively to HTML5, so Newgrounds, itch.io and CrazyGames each hosted their own optimized build, and being open source meant remixes needed no permission — the mod ecosystem became the content pipeline. Lars Doucet's read is that free-plus-web collapsed the friction to a single click, and the distribution reached channels the developer never anticipated. OSU!: 21.8M registered accounts growing ~500K/month, ~200K MAU, 100K+ peak concurrent — built entirely on community charting, which is a content engine CHOP cannot and should not replicate. PORTAL SCALE: Poki reports 100M MAU, 625M players in 2025 and 1B gameplays in a single month; CrazyGames ~40M MAU and 91.68M visits in one month at an average session of 15:08. HYPERCASUAL MUSIC: the category grosses over $10M/month; Amanotes alone has 2B downloads, 120M MAU across 50+ games, with Magic Tiles 3 at 810M downloads. RETENTION BENCHMARKS to judge CHOP against: hypercasual D1 20-30% / D7 5-10% / D30 <2%; casual puzzle D1 30-40% / D7 10-20% / D30 3-7%. CHOP's daily-block structure should target the casual-puzzle column, not the hypercasual one.
- CROSS-CUTTING PATTERN — every retention engine in this teardown is a COLLECTION, and every gate on that collection is resented. Beatstar collects song cards behind 3-hour timers; Cytus II collects character levels and iM posts behind XP grind; Arcaea collects Potential behind a 12-cap stamina meter and a ~£30 paywall; Phigros collects RKS behind nothing and posts the best ratings in the category. CHOP's THE CHAIN is a collection whose scarcity is externally imposed and free — blocks arrive roughly every ten minutes, each is sealable once, and the specific block you sealed is permanently yours. That is the Wordle structure (one appointment per interval, no binge, a streak you can break) running on a clock no operator has to fake and no player can resent, because bitcoin's block cadence is not a monetization decision. No competitor in this teardown can copy it. It is the strongest single asset CHOP has and v2 shipped without using it.
- CHOP'S CURRENT BUILD, AUDITED AGAINST THE ABOVE (files at <repo>/public/chop/). The v2 code confirms the rejection notes. Scoring at js/main.js:1143-1148 is `S.combo++; mult = 1 + min(combo,20)*0.08; gain = round(t.sats * mult)` — a pure Fruit Ninja combo-multiplier chain with no 115 decomposition, no per-cut quality term, no grade, and no kerf: grepping main.js for 'kerf' returns nothing, so directional cuts are designed but not yet in the build. The spawner comment at main.js:1300-1301 self-describes as 'WAVE grammar (Fruit Ninja)', which is exactly the 'too Fruit Ninja' verdict. There is no meta-progression code and no seal/grade surface, which is exactly 'no goal.' The block connection exists only as a HUD string ('SYNCING…' at index.html) and a fee-tint on slabs — not legible as the level's identity, which is exactly 'no explanation of the block connection.' Positively: the latency scaffolding, hitstop, haptics, per-swing directional ember spray (`burstAlong`), and adaptive music intensity tiers are all genuinely good and should survive the redesign. PAYLOAD NOTE: vendored Three.js is 385KB + 366KB raw (~190KB gzipped combined) plus 69KB main.js — heavy against web-portal norms where first-load speed gates discovery, though acceptable if brotli-served from the existing CDN.

## Recommendations

- #1 (HIGHEST IMPACT) — SCORE THE SWING, NOT THE MILLISECOND. Port Beat Saber's 115 decomposition to 2D touch and make timing a gate, never a score. Concretely: (a) timing becomes a single generous hit/miss window of roughly ±110-130ms — wide enough to survive uncompensated iOS output latency, and nothing else depends on it; (b) all 115 points resolve on geometry the browser measures perfectly — 70 for swipe-angle agreement with the kerf axis (full credit inside ~25°, falling to zero by ~60°), 30 for follow-through length measured as travel past the slab's far face, 15 for the perpendicular distance of the cut line from the slab's center; (c) keep the existing 1x→2x→4x→8x-style combo ladder but drive it off note count, not off score. Then fix the latency floor properly: music.js:84-97 currently falls through to 0 on every iPhone, so add a third rung — a 3-tap calibration ('chop the downbeat, three times') on first boot that measures the player's own audio-visual offset, persists to localStorage, and is re-runnable from settings. This single recommendation resolves the 'too hard' half of the v1 rejection (geometry is far more forgiving than timing), makes the kerf mechanic load-bearing instead of decorative, and turns the platform's worst constraint into CHOP's differentiator. Nothing else on this list matters if scoring stays timing-shaped.
- #2 — MAKE THE BLOCK THE LEVEL, VISIBLY, IN THE FIRST TEN SECONDS. v2 was rejected for 'no explanation of the block connection,' and the current build only surfaces it as a HUD string. Replace the boot sequence with one continuous, unskippable-but-fast (≈4s) move: the real block header assembles in the corridor — height, hash tail, transaction count, total fees, the timestamp — then physically SHATTERS into the note swarm the player is about to cut. No text panel, no tooltip; the explanation IS the animation. Then keep it legible throughout: each slab carries its own tx identity (fee rate in sat/vB drives its color and its point value, size drives its mass), the progress bar reads as transactions cleared rather than as an abstract percentage, and the results card states it plainly — 'BLOCK 908,412 · SEALED · you cleared 2,847 of 3,104 transactions · grade A.' The connection has to be something the player watches happen, not something they read.
- #3 — BUILD THE CHAIN AS FREE, REAL, PERMANENT SCARCITY (the fix for 'no goal, stale after seconds'). Every competitor gates its collection with a mechanic players resent: Beatstar's 3-hour cases with only 3 slots, Arcaea's stamina that caps at 12. CHOP should gate with the chain itself and nothing else. Rules: a block can be sealed exactly once, by anyone, forever; its grade is engraved on your chain permanently; unsealed blocks scroll away as new ones arrive. That produces Wordle's appointment-scarcity — always another one coming, never another one right now — on a clock no operator invented and no player can blame. Layer Cytus II's most important rule on top: award chain progress for EVERY play, including plays that set no record, so a session is never wasted. Surface the chain as the primary home screen (a growing ledger of your sealed blocks, gaps visible where you missed days), give it one portable number in the spirit of Arcaea's Potential — a rolling average grade across your last N seals, which rewards consistency rather than one lucky run — and add a streak. Target the casual-puzzle retention column (D1 30-40%, D7 10-20%) rather than the hypercasual one (D7 5-10%); the daily-appointment structure is what makes that reachable.
- #4 — AUTHOR THE CHARTS AS CHOREOGRAPHY, NOT AS BLOCK ENTROPY. Road to VR's core point is that arrows exist so players do not flail — the fun is the choreography. Real block data is entropy, and entropy charted directly is exactly what 'too Fruit Ninja, stale after seconds' feels like. Insert a pattern-grammar layer between the block and the spawner: define a small fixed vocabulary of 2-6 note phrases with authored kerf continuity (down-up ladder, cross-slash X, single-lane sweep, hold-and-turn, alternating-row zigzag), then use the block hash as a permutation seed to select and sequence phrases — never to place individual notes. This preserves the honest claim that the block determines the level while guaranteeing every level is playable and every phrase flows out of the previous swipe's exit vector. Simultaneously adopt Fruit Ninja's asymmetric-hitbox generosity, which is the specific reason a 2010 game still out-feels the field: make each slab's hit volume LARGER than its rendered mesh and each bomb/REORG hazard's trigger volume SMALLER. Keep the existing hitstop, haptics, and burstAlong directional spray — those are already correct and are the game's best-feeling code.
- #5 — SHIP A SPOILER-FREE, MEDIA-NATIVE SHARE ARTIFACT FOR EVERY FINISHER. Wordle's emoji grid solved the exact problem CHOP has: brag without spoiling, in a format that renders anywhere. Build the block-seal card as a native PNG: block height, grade, sats, the 3x2 lane heat-grid of where your chops landed, and the chain length. Two rules the repo has already learned the hard way in the kart funnel and must not relearn — fire the share for EVERY finisher rather than gating it on a good grade, and share the PNG as native media rather than as a link card. Add the dare vector that already works elsewhere in this arcade: a share link that boots the recipient straight into the SAME block, so the comparison is exact and the reply is a rematch. Because the shared block is a public, verifiable, permanently-timestamped object, a CHOP share is legible to bitcoin X in a way no rhythm-game score ever is — that is the audience fit, and it is worth more than any in-game viral mechanic.
- DO NOT COMPETE ON #1 — LICENSED MUSIC. Beatstar did it best: $73M in year one, $16M to rightsholders, 1M DAU, 38M downloads, artists releasing singles on it 48 hours ahead of streaming — and it shut down on 2025-10-31. Its most-hated features (no practice mode, 3-slot case gating, no unlimited play, $10-15 season pass) all existed to control per-play licensing cost, and Naavik's teardown ties them directly to that constraint. Phigros, by contrast, monetizes nothing and posts 4.5/5 and 4.8/5 store ratings. CHOP's code-generated soundtrack lets it offer unlimited free replay of every block forever, which is the single largest structural advantage available in this category. Never license a track; the procedural score is not a compromise, it is the moat.
- DO NOT COMPETE ON #2 — THE EXPERT DIFFICULTY CEILING. osu! has 21.8M accounts, ~500K new per month, and a decade of community charting; Arcaea ships 5 difficulty tiers rated 1-12+ with per-chart constants for sub-level precision; both are built on input hardware that reports true timestamps at 15-25ms windows. CHOP cannot reach that ceiling on mobile web — the input timestamps do not exist and the audio path is uncompensatable on iOS — and chasing it reproduces the v1 'too hard' rejection. Cap at three difficulties, and make the top tier about CONSISTENCY ACROSS MANY BLOCKS (your rolling chain average) rather than finger speed within one. Depth should come from the ledger, not from the chart.
- DO NOT COMPETE ON #3 — CONTENT VOLUME AND THE SONG LIBRARY. Phigros ships 200+ tracks, Beatstar 300+, Muse Dash 40+ base with a ~$30 DLC that its own community complains about, and Amanotes' Magic Tiles 3 has 810M downloads on a catalog CHOP could never match. Do not build a song-select screen and do not build a track roster — both would invite exactly the comparison CHOP loses. CHOP's variety argument is categorically different and stronger: a brand-new, never-before-played level materializes roughly every ten minutes, forever, free, generated by a public ledger. One soundtrack that adapts to combo intensity (which the current music.js already does at three tiers) plus an infinite supply of levels beats a finite library of songs, but only if the product never frames itself as a music game with too few songs.
